## Is the Bitcoin Four-Year Cycle Over?

Not yet, at least not on the evidence so far. Bitcoin's timing in 2024-2026 has followed the four-year cycle almost to the month: it peaked on 6 October 2025, 534 days after the April 2024 halving, and then fell by roughly half. What has changed is the depth of the fall, not the rhythm, and that is exactly the point the two camps are arguing about.

The question is suddenly urgent. Bitcoin slid to about $62,000 in mid-November 2026, its weakest level since the summer, after six straight weekly losses and more than $2 billion in liquidations over a single weekend. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index dropped to around 13, deep in "extreme fear". A year ago, many of the biggest names in crypto were saying the old cycle was dead and that 2026 would bring new highs. So far, 2026 has looked a lot more like the old script.

This guide explains what the four-year cycle actually is, how the last three cycles played out, what has been different this time, and the strongest arguments on each side. You will also see what the debate means in practice if you hold, or are thinking about buying, **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)**.

## What Is the Bitcoin Four-Year Cycle?

The four-year cycle is the pattern Bitcoin's price has followed since 2012: a big rally in the 12 to 18 months after each halving, a peak, a crash of roughly 75% to 85% over the following year, and then a long, quiet rebuilding phase before the next halving starts it all again. The halving is the anchor, which is why the pattern repeats roughly every four years.

A **[Bitcoin halving](/posts/the-bitcoin-halving)** cuts the number of new coins miners receive for each block in half. It happens every 210,000 blocks, which works out to about every four years. The reward started at 50 BTC in 2009, dropped to 25 in 2012, 12.5 in 2016, 6.25 in 2020 and 3.125 in April 2024. The next halving, which cuts it to 1.5625 BTC, is expected around April 2028.

The theory behind the cycle is simple supply and demand. Miners sell a large share of the coins they earn to pay for electricity and hardware. When their reward is cut in half overnight, the daily flow of fresh coins hitting the market shrinks, while demand stays the same or grows. Over the following months, that squeeze pushes the price up, the rising price attracts new buyers and media attention, and the rally feeds on itself until it overheats.

There is a second, less mechanical explanation that matters just as much: the cycle works partly because people believe in it. Traders who expect a peak about 18 months after a halving tend to sell around that point, and traders who expect a bottom about a year later tend to buy then. A widely shared expectation can become a self-fulfilling prophecy, which is also why some analysts think the pattern could fade once enough people trade against it.

## How the Previous Cycles Played Out

The striking thing about the past cycles is how consistent the timing has been, even as the size of the swings has shrunk. Here is the record, using approximate prices, since different data providers average different exchanges:

| Halving | Cycle peak | Days from halving to peak | Following low | Fall from peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 28 Nov 2012 | ~$1,150 (late 2013) | ~370 | ~$152 (Jan 2015) | ~87% |
| 9 Jul 2016 | ~$19,800 (Dec 2017) | ~525 | ~$3,200 (Dec 2018) | ~84% |
| 11 May 2020 | ~$69,000 (Nov 2021) | ~550 | ~$15,500 (Nov 2022) | ~77% |
| 20 Apr 2024 | ~$126,000 (6 Oct 2025) | 534 | ~$58,000 so far (Jun 2026) | ~54% so far |

Two patterns stand out. From 2016 onward, the peak arrived between 17 and 18 months after the halving every time, and the bottom came roughly 12 months after the peak. At the same time, each crash was a little shallower than the one before: 87%, then 84%, then 77%.

The 2022 bottom is a useful reminder of how these bear phases feel from the inside. The low came in November 2022, right after the collapse of FTX, when a wave of forced selling and lost trust hit an already falling market. If you want the wider picture of what those phases look like, our guide to the **[bear market in crypto](/posts/bear-market-in-crypto)** walks through the warning signs and the typical stages.

## What Happened This Time

The 2024 cycle started with an important twist. In January 2024, three months before the halving, US regulators approved spot Bitcoin ETFs, and billions of dollars of institutional money flowed in. For the first time, Bitcoin set a new all-time high before its halving, reaching about $73,000 in March 2024. That broke one of the cycle's oldest rules and was the first big reason people started calling the pattern dead.

After the halving, though, things went more or less to plan. Bitcoin climbed through 2025 and topped out at about $126,000 on 6 October 2025, 534 days after the halving, right in the 17 to 18-month window of the previous two cycles. From there it fell steadily, broke below $60,000 in early June 2026 for the first time since late 2024, and reached a low near $58,000, about 54% below the peak.

<ArticleAd />

A recovery followed. Bitcoin was back around $76,000 by mid-September 2026 and briefly above $80,000 in early November. Then the November sell-off took it back to roughly $62,000 to $64,000 by 17 November 2026, about 50% below the record. US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw around $1.9 billion in net outflows during that sell-off, according to Crypto Briefing, and **[crypto liquidations](/posts/what-is-a-crypto-liquidation)** of leveraged positions sped up the drop.

Put the timeline next to the history and the match is uncomfortable for the "cycle is dead" camp. A peak about 18 months after the halving, followed by a bear market that is now about 13 months old: that is close to what the old model predicted. The one clear difference is depth. A 54% drop is painful, but it is far from the 77% to 87% wipeouts of earlier cycles.

## The Case That the Cycle Is Over

The most common argument is that Bitcoin's buyers have changed. Spot **[crypto ETFs](/posts/crypto-etfs-explained)**, pension funds and public companies now hold a large share of the supply, and they tend to buy on a schedule rather than in a frenzy. Nick Ruck of LVRG Research argued in December 2025 that this steady institutional demand had already reduced volatility and softened the post-peak crash compared with earlier cycles.

Corporate treasuries are part of the same story. Strategy, the company run by **[Michael Saylor](/crypto-ogs/michael-saylor)**, kept buying through the downturn, including 485 BTC for about $33.4 million in the week of 9 to 15 November 2026. Buyers like this do not panic-sell at the first sign of weakness, which in theory puts a floor under the price that earlier cycles never had.

The second argument is that the halving itself has become too small to matter. When the reward fell from 50 to 25 BTC in 2012, new supply was a big share of the coins in circulation. Today, with about 20 million BTC already mined, the 3.125 BTC reward adds only around 450 BTC a day. On some days ETFs alone have bought or sold several times that amount, which makes the halving a small factor next to institutional flows.

The third argument is about macro conditions. Some analysts think Bitcoin never really followed the halving, but followed global liquidity and the business cycle instead, and that the two just happened to line up. Raoul Pal of Real Vision has argued for a roughly five-year cycle tied to the economy, while **[Arthur Hayes](/crypto-ogs/arthur-hayes)** has long said Bitcoin moves with central bank money printing rather than with block rewards. Mid-November's sell-off was widely blamed on rising expectations of a US interest rate hike, which fits that view.

## The Case That the Cycle Is Still Alive

The strongest argument for the cycle is the simplest: it keeps showing up. Many of the voices calling it dead were also predicting a new high in 2026. In December 2025, Grayscale forecast a new all-time high in the first half of 2026 and the end of the four-year cycle, and Standard Chartered still expected $150,000 by the end of 2026. Instead, Bitcoin spent the first half of 2026 falling to its June low.

Some analysts saw this coming using the old model. Fidelity's director of global macro, Jurrien Timmer, argued in late 2025 that 2026 could be a weaker year, in line with the historical pattern for that stage of the cycle. Markus Thielen of 10x Research said in December 2025 that Bitcoin had entered a bear market in late October 2025. Both calls have aged better than the "supercycle" forecasts.

There is also a middle position, and it is probably the most honest one. The cycle may not be dead, just maturing. Each crash has been shallower than the last, and this one, so far, is the shallowest yet. If institutional buyers are dampening the swings rather than removing them, the cycle would look exactly like this: the same timing, but with smaller peaks and smaller drawdowns. As the analyst Rekt Capital put it in December 2025, a "broken" cycle may simply be a cycle "leveling up".

## What to Watch Next

If the old pattern holds, the bottom of this bear market should arrive roughly 12 months after the October 2025 peak, which puts it somewhere around late 2026, possibly the period we are living through right now. The following phase would then be a slow recovery through 2027 ahead of the April 2028 halving. The first test is whether the June 2026 low near $58,000 holds; market makers cited by Crypto Briefing in November 2026 named $60,000 to $62,000 as the key support zone.

If the price breaks well below that and keeps falling, the drawdown would start to look like a classic crypto winter, and the cycle camp would be vindicated a second time. If it holds and the market recovers early, without the long sideways grind of 2015, 2019 or 2023, that would be the clearest sign yet that the pattern really has changed.

Remember that the price is not set by a calendar. Large holders, ETFs, miners and leveraged traders all push it around, as our guide on **[who controls Bitcoin's price](/posts/who-controls-bitcoins-price)** explains. Tools like the **[Bitcoin Rainbow Chart](/tools/bitcoin-rainbow-chart)** can help you see where the price sits against its long-term history, but no chart can tell you the exact bottom.

## What This Means for You

The honest answer is that nobody knows whether the cycle is over, and you do not need to know in order to make sensible decisions. If you are investing for several years, strategies that do not depend on timing are your best protection against being wrong. **[Dollar-cost averaging](/posts/what-is-dollar-cost-averaging)**, buying a fixed amount at regular intervals, means you buy more coins when prices are low and fewer when they are high, without having to call the bottom.

Leverage is where the cycle debate becomes dangerous. Traders who borrowed heavily on the belief that the cycle was dead and 2026 would bring new highs were among those wiped out this year. If you are tempted to bet on the next move, remember that even a correct view of the cycle can lose you money if the timing is off by a few months. Bitcoin's **[volatility](/posts/crypto-volatility)** is the one feature every cycle has had in common.

Finally, watch your own emotions as closely as the chart. Extreme fear readings like November 2026's have historically appeared near major lows, while extreme greed has tended to appear near peaks. Our **[Fear and Greed Index](/tools/fear-and-greed-index)** tracks that sentiment daily, and it is worth checking before you make a big decision in either direction.

## Conclusion: Changed, Not Dead (So Far)

The four-year cycle has not ended yet. The 2024-2026 cycle peaked on schedule, 534 days after the halving, and has spent the 13 months since in a bear market, just as the old model predicted. What has changed is how hard it falls: a drop of about 54% so far, compared with 77% to 87% in earlier cycles, as ETFs and corporate buyers make Bitcoin a larger, less extreme market.

The real test is still ahead. If the market bottoms around now and recovers into the 2028 halving, the cycle will have survived its biggest challenge in a softer form. If it breaks the pattern in either direction, the debate will finally have an answer. Either way, you do not need to predict it to invest sensibly.
