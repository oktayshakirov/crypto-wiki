## **GTA 6 and Crypto: The Short Answer**

**Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA 6)** will not use real cryptocurrency, as far as anything Rockstar Games has announced shows: there are no confirmed Bitcoin payments, wallets, tokens, NFTs or **[Blockchain](/posts/what-is-blockchain)** features in the game. What it does have is a fictional coin. Rockstar's launch trailer, released on 15 October 2026, shows a stunt plane painted with "DacityCoin AudaCity", a made-up crypto brand that fits the series' long habit of mocking whatever America is obsessed with.

The game is set to launch on **19 November 2026** on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with no PC date announced as of October 2026. It is set in the fictional state of Leonida, home to a modern Vice City, and follows two protagonists, Lucia and Jason. The date has moved twice: Rockstar first pushed it from fall 2025 to 26 May 2026 (announced May 2025), then to 19 November 2026 (announced November 2025). Take-Two has repeatedly said since then that the November date holds.

This article was first written in 2024, when everything about GTA 6 and crypto was rumor. A month before launch, there is enough on the record to separate the satire, the rumors and the outright scams.

**Disclaimer:** _Rockstar Games and Take-Two Interactive have made no announcement of cryptocurrency, blockchain or NFT features in GTA 6. Anything below that is not attributed to them is reporting, leaks or analysis, and is labelled as such._

## **Dacity Coin: The Crypto Rockstar Actually Showed**

The clearest crypto reference Rockstar has published itself is a two-second shot in the 15 October 2026 launch trailer. At around the 55-second mark, a plane carrying the words "DacityCoin AudaCity" flies past while a character lights a pink flare on its wing.

What it tells us:

- **It is fictional.** AudaCity and Dacity Coin are invented brands inside the game world, in the same way GTA V invented LifeInvader to parody Facebook and ran fake stock exchanges (BAWSAQ and LCN) instead of real ones.
- **It is satire, not a feature.** Nothing in the trailer suggests players will earn, hold or trade Dacity Coin. Fan sites built on leaked footage describe a storyline in which Lucia is hired to promote the AudaCity brand with skydiving stunts, which would make the coin a plot device about crypto hype rather than a currency. Rockstar has not confirmed any of that.
- **It fits the setting.** A Miami-inspired city full of influencers, nightlife and fast money is exactly where a GTA satire of meme coin culture would land.

Within hours, real traders tried to cash in. A **[Solana](/posts/what-is-solana)** token called Dacity, launched on the Pump.fun platform on 11 October 2026, four days before the trailer, jumped from a market cap of roughly $104,000 to about $3.18 million in the hour after the trailer dropped, a gain of around 2,900% according to figures reported by KuCoin News and others. It then lost roughly half of that peak within days. Rockstar has no connection to the token and has not endorsed it.

## **From "Missions Pay in Bitcoin" to "Scrapped Years Ago"**

The idea that GTA 6 would feature real crypto traces back mostly to one rumor. In June 2021, gaming reporter Tom Henderson posted that he had heard some GTA 6 missions would reward players in **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** instead of cash, and that the in-game stock market would return with a broker for digital currencies. He clarified at the time that the coin would probably not be called Bitcoin.

That single claim drove years of headlines. In February 2026, asked about it again, Henderson said he believed the feature had been "scrapped years ago." No leak, trailer or official reveal since has shown mission rewards paid in crypto, and the most specific crypto element seen so far is the purely fictional Dacity Coin.

Other speculation from the rumor years, such as tradable NFT cars or houses, or rewards in **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**, never had a credible source behind it.

## **Rockstar's Only Official Crypto Policy: The 2022 Roleplay Ban**

Rockstar has said almost nothing about crypto in its own games, with one important exception. In November 2022, it updated its rules for fan-run GTA roleplay servers to **ban the use of cryptocurrency and NFTs**, along with schemes that let players buy loot boxes or in-game currency for real money. Servers built around NFT sales were pushed offline shortly afterwards.

That is the only on-record crypto position Rockstar has taken, and nothing it has said since has reversed it. It also fits the wider industry mood: after the 2021 NFT boom, several big publishers tested in-game NFTs and ran into heavy player backlash, and the market for gaming NFTs collapsed alongside the rest of the sector (see **[Are NFTs Dead?](/posts/are-nfts-dead)**). For a studio whose biggest asset is the goodwill of hundreds of millions of players, real crypto in GTA 6 would have been a large risk for little upside.

## **The GTA 6 Leak Memecoin: A Real Scam Around a Fake Coin**

The most important crypto story around GTA 6 so far is not inside the game at all. In August 2026, an anonymous leaker going by **CYBERLEEK** began posting unauthorized GTA 6 footage, and tied the leaks to a memecoin of the same name on Pump.fun.

- **The setup:** The token launched on 15 August 2026, days before the first clip appeared on 18 August. Each clip came with the ticker on screen and the promise that a higher market cap would mean more leaks.
- **The pump:** The coin peaked at a market cap of roughly $25 million on 23 August 2026, according to figures reported by CoinDesk and Kotaku.
- **The exit:** On 27 August 2026, hours before Rockstar's own official gameplay showcase, wallets linked to the creator sold an estimated $250,000 or more, and the price fell by about half within a day.
- **The legal response:** Take-Two went to federal court in New York from 20 August 2026 seeking subpoenas to Microsoft, Discord and X to identify the leaker. Rockstar called the leaks "heartbreaking for our team." As of mid-October 2026, no one has been publicly named or charged.

It is a textbook **[Rug Pull](/posts/what-is-a-rug-pull)** pattern: attach a token to a hugely popular brand, promise more of what fans want if the price goes up, and sell into the hype. The same playbook was used in 2021 with the token named after a Netflix show, covered in **[The Squid Game Crypto Scam](/posts/the-squid-game-crypto-scam)**.

## **How to Stay Safe Around GTA 6 Launch Week**

Expect more of this as 19 November gets closer. Any coin, airdrop or "presale" using the GTA, Rockstar, Vice City or Dacity name is unofficial. Some practical rules:

- **Rockstar sells no crypto.** There is no official GTA token, no Dacity Coin you can buy and no NFT pre-order bonus. Anything claiming otherwise is a scam or a speculative bet on hype.
- **Trailer pumps fade fast.** The real Dacity token rose about 2,900% in an hour and gave back roughly half within days. **[Memecoins](/posts/what-are-memecoins)** that ride news have nothing behind them once the news cycle moves on.
- **Watch the wallet links.** Fake "GTA 6 beta", "free download" and "early access" offers are a common way to get people to connect a wallet or sign a malicious transaction.
- **Check the numbers.** A small token's **[Market Cap](/posts/what-is-market-cap-in-crypto)** can be pushed up by a handful of buyers, so a big percentage jump says little about real demand.

For a broader checklist, see **[How to Avoid Crypto Scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)**.

## **Why Real Crypto in GTA 6 Was Never Likely**

Even setting aside the 2022 ban, building real crypto into a game of this size runs into problems that have not gone away since this article was first written:

1.  **Volatility:** Tying rewards or item values to real coins would make the game economy swing with the market. A crash could wipe out players' progress, and a rally could break game balance. **[Crypto Volatility](/posts/crypto-volatility)** is a feature for traders and a bug for game design.
2.  **Regulation:** Anything with real-world value turns a game into a financial product, subject to securities, gambling and money transmission rules that differ in every country GTA 6 is sold in.
3.  **Security:** Valuable, tradable items attract theft, phishing and exploits, and the people targeted would be ordinary players, many of them young.
4.  **Player trust:** Most GTA players are not interested in crypto, and earlier NFT experiments by big publishers showed how quickly a community turns on the idea.
5.  **The money is already there:** GTA V's online mode earned billions from in-game currency sold directly by Rockstar. A closed, developer-controlled economy is simpler and more profitable than an open one.

**[GameFi](/posts/what-is-gamefi)** projects such as Axie Infinity showed that play-to-earn economies can grow fast, but also that they tend to collapse when new players stop arriving. Nothing suggests Rockstar wants to bet its biggest release on that model.

## **What GTA 6 Could Still Mean for Crypto**

GTA 6 is likely to be one of the biggest entertainment launches ever, and its satire reaches people who never read crypto news. If Dacity Coin turns out to be part of the story, millions of players will see crypto hype, influencer promotion and pump-and-dump culture played for laughs, which is arguably a more honest introduction to the space than most marketing.

It is also a reminder that crypto and gaming have moved apart rather than together since the 2021 peak of **[Web3](/posts/what-is-web3)** gaming. The biggest game of the decade looks set to treat crypto as something to joke about, not something to build on.

## **Conclusion: Satire Yes, Real Crypto No**

A month before launch, the picture is much clearer than it was in 2024. Rockstar has shown a fictional coin, Dacity Coin, as part of its satire. It has confirmed no real cryptocurrency, blockchain or NFT feature, and its only official crypto policy is a 2022 ban on crypto and NFTs in fan-run roleplay servers. The long-running Bitcoin rumor was, by its own source's account, dropped years ago.

The real crypto risk around GTA 6 is outside the game: memecoins and scams borrowing its name, from the CYBERLEEK leak token to the trailer-pumped Dacity copycat. Enjoy the game on 19 November, and treat any coin with GTA in its pitch as what it almost certainly is, a bet on hype with no connection to Rockstar.
