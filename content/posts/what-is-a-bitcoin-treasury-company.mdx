## What Is a Bitcoin Treasury Company?

A bitcoin treasury company is a publicly listed business that holds bitcoin as its main reserve asset and raises money from investors, through new shares, loans or preferred stock, mainly to buy more of it. Buying the stock is effectively buying a slice of a bitcoin pile run by a management team, with debt and dilution layered on top.

The model has grown fast. As of 22 November 2026, 173 public companies held a combined 1,289,321 BTC, about 6.13% of the 21 million coins that will ever exist, according to the tracker Trinity Insights (other trackers count slightly different totals). One company, Strategy, holds roughly two-thirds of that on its own.

It is also under real stress right now. **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** fell below $59,000 on 20 November 2026, less than half its October 2025 record of about $126,000, and several treasury companies have started selling coins they once promised to hold forever. This guide explains how the model works, what the key metric "mNAV" means, why the selling started and what it means for you.

## How It Started: Strategy and the Copycats

The idea began with one software company. On 11 August 2020, MicroStrategy, a business-intelligence firm, announced it had bought 21,454 BTC for $250 million and would treat bitcoin as its primary treasury reserve instead of cash. Its co-founder and executive chairman, **[Michael Saylor](/crypto-ogs/michael-saylor)**, argued that cash lost value to inflation every year while bitcoin's supply was fixed.

Other companies followed in smaller steps. Tesla, led by **[Elon Musk](/crypto-ogs/elon-musk)**, disclosed a $1.5 billion bitcoin purchase in February 2021, which at the time was the biggest corporate vote of confidence the asset had ever had. Bitcoin miners also kept growing stacks of the coins they produced, partly because holding looked smarter than selling during a bull market.

The real boom came in 2025. MicroStrategy renamed itself Strategy in February 2025, and dozens of small listed companies, from Metaplanet, a former Japanese hotel operator that began buying in 2024, to shell companies with little other business, copied the playbook. The model also spread beyond Bitcoin. BitMine Immersion, chaired by the Wall Street strategist **[Tom Lee](/crypto-ogs/tom-lee)**, built the largest **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)** treasury, with 6,077,745 ETH as of mid-November 2026, about 4.97% of all ether, most of it staked.

Strategy still dwarfs everyone. Its 8-K filing for the week ending 15 November 2026 shows 850,849 BTC, bought for a total of $64.20 billion at an average of $75,452 per coin. Even that week, with the market falling, it added 485 BTC for $33.4 million.

## How a Bitcoin Treasury Company Works

A normal company earns money by selling products and spends what is left. A treasury company runs a different engine: it sells pieces of itself to investors and turns the money into bitcoin. Most of them use a mix of three tools.

- **New shares:** the company sells fresh stock into the market, often through an "at-the-market" program that drips shares out daily, and buys bitcoin with the cash.
- **Convertible bonds:** it borrows money at a low interest rate, and lenders get the right to swap the debt for shares later if the stock rises.
- **Preferred stock:** it sells shares that pay a fixed dividend, a kind of hybrid between a bond and equity. Strategy's STRC preferred, for example, pays a 12% annual rate as of November 2026.

The goal is not to own as much bitcoin as possible in total, but to grow the amount of bitcoin behind each share. Strategy calls this "BTC yield". If the number of coins grows faster than the number of shares, existing shareholders end up owning more bitcoin per share than before, even though they never bought another share themselves.

That trick only works under one condition: the stock has to trade above the value of the bitcoin the company holds. That is where mNAV comes in.

## What Is mNAV and Why Does It Matter?

mNAV stands for "multiple of net asset value". It compares what the stock market says the company is worth with what its bitcoin is worth. You divide the company's **[market cap](/posts/what-is-market-cap-in-crypto)** by the value of its bitcoin holdings. An mNAV of 2 means investors pay $2 for every $1 of bitcoin inside the company. An mNAV of 0.8 means the bitcoin is on sale at 80 cents on the dollar. (Some trackers use "enterprise mNAV", which also adds the company's debt and preferred stock, so the figures you see quoted can differ.)

Here is why the number decides everything. Imagine a company with 100 million shares and 10,000 BTC, with bitcoin at $60,000.

If the stock trades at an mNAV of 1.5, the company is worth $900 million and each share costs $9. It sells $100 million of new shares, about 11.1 million of them, and buys roughly 1,667 BTC. It now owns 11,667 BTC across 111.1 million shares, so the bitcoin behind each share has grown by about 5%. Every existing holder is better off.

Now take the same company at an mNAV of 0.8. Each share costs only $4.80, so raising the same $100 million takes about 20.8 million new shares. After buying the same 1,667 BTC, it owns 11,667 BTC across 120.8 million shares, and the bitcoin behind each share has fallen by about 3%. Every existing holder is worse off.

A premium is a growth engine, and a discount runs that engine in reverse. When mNAV is high, issuing shares to buy bitcoin is "accretive", the industry's word for adding value per share. When mNAV drops below 1, the only way to raise bitcoin per share is the opposite move: sell bitcoin and buy back shares. That is exactly what has started to happen.

## Why Treasury Companies Started Selling in 2026

The premiums faded as the market fell. Strategy's enterprise mNAV slipped below 1 for the first time on 26 June 2026, according to The Block, and by 20 November 2026 The Motley Fool put it at about 0.98. Strategy's management has said selling bitcoin would be a last resort, considered only if mNAV stays below 1 and other sources of money run out. So far, it has kept buying in small amounts while holding $4.75 billion in a US dollar reserve, as of 15 November 2026, to cover dividends and interest.

Others did not wait. A few cases show the different reasons a "never sell" company ends up selling:

- **Paying back debt:** MARA, one of the largest bitcoin miners, disclosed on 9 November 2026 that it had sold 13,597 BTC for about $1.1 billion and used $750 million of it to repay two bitcoin-backed loans taken in August 2026. It said it plans to keep using bitcoin to fund AI data centers, convertible-note obligations and share buybacks.
- **Buying back cheap stock:** GD Culture Group approved liquidating its entire 7,500 BTC treasury in early March 2026 to fund share buybacks, a move that only makes sense when the stock trades below the value of the coins.
- **Reshuffling:** Metaplanet sold 10,000 BTC and bought 11,000 BTC during the third quarter of 2026, ending at 44,000 BTC on 30 September 2026 but booking a loss of about $122 million on the coins it sold. It is still far from its earlier goal of 100,000 BTC by the end of 2026.

MARA's story ties into the wider shift of miners toward AI, which our article on **[why Wall Street is betting on Bitcoin miners for AI](/posts/why-wall-street-is-betting-big-on-bitcoin-miners-for-ai)** covers in detail. The common thread across all three is simple: when the stock no longer trades at a premium, the bitcoin stack stops being a growth engine and becomes a pile of assets that can be sold to fix the balance sheet.

## The MSCI Index Decision

A second blow came from index providers. Index funds that track benchmarks like those run by MSCI must buy the companies inside them, which gave large treasury companies a steady pool of passive buyers. In October 2025, MSCI proposed excluding companies whose digital-asset holdings make up 50% or more of their total assets. More than 250 organizations objected, and in January 2026 MSCI held off.

The pause did not last. On 15 October 2026, MSCI finalized a new test for "non-operating" companies, based on their operating assets and five financial ratios, effective from its November review. On 11 November 2026 it published the result: Strategy will be removed from the MSCI USA Index and Metaplanet from the MSCI Japan Small Cap Index at the close of 30 November 2026, while the Ethereum treasury SharpLink was placed on a watch list.

Index removal does not change how much bitcoin these companies own, but it does force funds that track those indexes to sell the shares. That pressure lands on a stock already trading below the value of its bitcoin, which is why this decision matters for the mNAV story above.

## What This Means for You

If you are thinking about buying a treasury company stock instead of bitcoin itself, be clear about what you are adding. You get bitcoin exposure plus company risk: management decisions, debt that must be repaid, preferred dividends that must be paid, dilution from new shares and now index rules. On the way up, leverage and a premium can make the stock rise faster than bitcoin. On the way down, the same features make it fall harder, which is the classic pattern behind most **[crypto liquidations](/posts/what-is-a-crypto-liquidation)**.

If you only want the price of bitcoin, there are simpler routes. Spot **[crypto ETFs](/posts/crypto-etfs-explained)** hold the coins directly and trade close to their net asset value, so you never pay a premium of 2x for $1 of bitcoin. Buying the coins yourself and keeping them in your own wallet removes the middleman entirely.

If you still want to own a treasury company, check three numbers before you buy. Look at the current mNAV, since paying well above 1 means you are betting the premium will hold. Look at the debt and preferred stock, and when the bonds come due. And look at whether the company has a real business generating cash, or only the bitcoin pile. A habit of **[doing your own research](/posts/do-your-own-research)** matters more here than with almost any other crypto product.

## Conclusion: A Bitcoin Bet With Extra Moving Parts

A bitcoin treasury company is a listed business that raises money to buy and hold bitcoin, and its stock is a leveraged, managed wrapper around that pile of coins. The model works brilliantly when its shares trade above the value of its bitcoin, because every new share sold adds bitcoin per share. It stalls, or runs in reverse, when that premium disappears, and in 2026 it did: Strategy's mNAV fell below 1, MARA and others sold coins, and MSCI is pushing Strategy and Metaplanet out of its indexes on 30 November 2026.

None of this means the companies will collapse, and Strategy is still buying. But it does mean you should treat these stocks as their own kind of risk, not as a safer version of bitcoin.
