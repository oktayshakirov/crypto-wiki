## Which Billionaires Own Crypto?

The billionaires who own crypto fall into two groups. The first made their fortunes from crypto itself: Binance founder Changpeng Zhao, Tether's Giancarlo Devasini and Paolo Ardoino, Ripple's Chris Larsen, Coinbase's Brian Armstrong and Strategy's Michael Saylor. The second were already rich and bought in: Elon Musk, Jack Dorsey, Pavel Durov, Peter Thiel, Ray Dalio and Donald Trump.

The gap between the two groups is enormous. Forbes put Changpeng Zhao at about $110 billion on its 2026 World's Billionaires list, published in March 2026, which made him the 17th richest person on the planet. Most of the investors in the second group, by contrast, hold crypto as a small slice of fortunes built on cars, software, hedge funds or real estate.

This guide walks through both lists, explains where each fortune actually comes from, and shows why these numbers swing so wildly from one month to the next. Along the way you will see what the richest people in crypto really own, and what their bets can and cannot teach an ordinary investor.

## Two Kinds of Crypto Billionaire

A crypto-made billionaire usually did not get rich by buying coins early and waiting. Most of the biggest fortunes come from owning the companies that sit in the middle of the market: exchanges that take a fee on every trade, and stablecoin issuers that earn interest on the dollars backing their tokens. Holding coins alone has produced far fewer ten-figure fortunes than owning the businesses around them.

The investor group works the other way around. Their wealth came from somewhere else, and crypto shows up as a corporate treasury, a fund position or a personal allocation. For them, a crypto crash is a bad quarter. For a founder whose entire net worth sits in one exchange or token, it can wipe out half their fortune in weeks.

One caveat applies to every figure below. Binance and Tether are private companies, so their founders' fortunes rest on estimates of what those companies would be worth if they were sold. Different trackers make different assumptions and reach very different numbers, which is why every figure here carries the date it was published.

## The Billionaires Crypto Created

**[Changpeng Zhao](/crypto-ogs/changpeng-zhao)**, known as CZ, is by any measure the richest person crypto has produced. Forbes estimated his fortune at about $110 billion as of 1 March 2026, based on a stake in Binance it puts at around 90%, and its live profile showed $114.6 billion on 3 November 2026. That made him richer than Bill Gates on paper, despite the four months he served in a US prison in 2024 after pleading guilty to anti-money-laundering failures. Binance's own valuation drives almost all of it.

The second-largest crypto fortune belongs to someone most crypto users have never heard of. Giancarlo Devasini, Tether's chairman and an estimated 45% shareholder, was worth $89.3 billion on the 2026 Forbes list, making him the richest person in Italy. Tether's CEO **[Paolo Ardoino](/crypto-ogs/paolo-ardoino)**, with an estimated stake of around 19%, was valued at $38 billion. Both fortunes come from USDT, the largest of all **[stablecoins](/posts/what-are-stablecoins)**, whose issuer earns billions a year in interest on the US Treasuries backing it. Forbes based those numbers on a Tether valuation of around $200 billion.

Further down the list, the founders of public crypto companies are easier to value because their shares trade every day. In a February 2026 tally of crypto crash losses, Forbes put Ripple co-founder Chris Larsen at $10.2 billion and Coinbase CEO **[Brian Armstrong](/crypto-ogs/brian-armstrong)** at $7.5 billion, with Armstrong's wealth tied mainly to his stake of roughly 19% in Coinbase. TRON founder **[Justin Sun](/crypto-ogs/justin-sun)** joined the Forbes list in 2025 with an estimated $8.5 billion.

The newest member of the club is Circle CEO **[Jeremy Allaire](/crypto-ogs/jeremy-allaire)**. Circle, the company behind the USDC stablecoin, listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2025, and its shares jumped so fast that Forbes estimated Allaire's fortune at a minimum of $1.7 billion within days. His story shows how quickly a stock listing can turn years of paper value into a measurable fortune.

## The Believers Who Got Rich Holding It

A smaller group did get rich mainly by holding coins, usually after betting early and refusing to sell. **[Michael Saylor](/crypto-ogs/michael-saylor)** is the best-known example. He turned MicroStrategy, now called Strategy, into a company that exists largely to buy bitcoin, and he said in 2020 that he also personally held about 17,732 BTC. Forbes valued him at $4.6 billion in February 2026, after the market fell sharply from its October 2025 peak.

Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss followed a similar path. The twins bought bitcoin in 2012, when a single coin cost around $10, using part of their settlement with Facebook, and later founded the Gemini exchange. Forbes put each of them at $3.5 billion in February 2026.

**[Vitalik Buterin](/crypto-ogs/vitalik-buterin)** shows how fragile this kind of fortune is. When Ether crossed $3,000 in May 2021, his public wallet held 333,521 ETH worth about $1.13 billion, and Forbes called the then 27-year-old the youngest crypto billionaire in the world. He has since given large amounts away, and on-chain tracker Arkham estimated his known holdings at about $536 million in May 2026.

Then there is the billionaire nobody can name. Blockchain analysis of Bitcoin's earliest blocks suggests **[Satoshi Nakamoto](/crypto-ogs/satoshi-nakamoto)** mined roughly 1 million BTC in 2009 and 2010, coins that have never moved. At almost any price of the last five years that stash would rank among the world's largest fortunes, yet it appears on no list, because no one can prove who controls it.

## Billionaires Who Invested in Crypto

**[Elon Musk](/crypto-ogs/elon-musk)**, whom Forbes called the richest person ever recorded in March 2026, is the most famous billionaire with crypto exposure. Tesla announced a $1.5 billion bitcoin purchase in February 2021, sold roughly 75% of it in 2022, and on-chain trackers estimated its remaining holding at around 11,500 BTC in early 2026. Musk said in July 2021 that he personally owns bitcoin, ether and dogecoin, and his posts about Dogecoin have moved its price more than once. Even so, crypto is a rounding error next to his stakes in Tesla and SpaceX.

**[Jack Dorsey](/crypto-ogs/jack-dorsey)** went much further. After co-founding Twitter, he rebuilt his payments company Block around bitcoin. Block disclosed 28,355 BTC as of March 2026, about 8,997 BTC of it in its own treasury and the rest held for Cash App customers, and it builds bitcoin mining chips and self-custody hardware.

Telegram founder **[Pavel Durov](/crypto-ogs/pavel-durov)** has said in interviews that he bought his first few thousand bitcoin in 2013 at roughly $700 each, and that bitcoin, not Telegram, funded his lifestyle for years. Telegram itself is closely tied to the TON blockchain, which makes him both an investor and a builder.

Peter Thiel, the PayPal co-founder, has backed crypto mostly through his venture firm Founders Fund. In July 2025 Founders Fund entities disclosed a 9.1% stake in BitMine, the Ether treasury company chaired by Tom Lee, and the firm also invested in ETHZilla before selling out by the end of 2025. Thiel is also a backer of Bullish, the exchange that listed in New York in 2025.

## The Converts: Dalio, Tudor Jones and Trump

Some of the most telling names are billionaires who were skeptical first. Hedge fund founder Ray Dalio long preferred gold, but in July 2025 he suggested investors hold about 15% of their portfolio in gold or bitcoin as a hedge against US debt, and in November 2025 he said on CNBC that around 1% of his own portfolio is in bitcoin. Paul Tudor Jones made headlines in May 2020 when he called bitcoin a hedge against inflation, and in 2021 he said he wanted about 5% of his assets in it.

The most dramatic conversion belongs to **[Donald Trump](/crypto-ogs/donald-trump)**, who called bitcoin a scam in 2021. Forbes estimated his net worth at about $6.5 billion in March 2026 and counted roughly $2.1 billion of it in crypto holdings and cash, including the TRUMP meme coin, his family's stake in World Liberty Financial and its USD1 stablecoin. Crypto went from zero to the largest new source of his wealth in about two years, which has also made it the most politically controversial.

## Why Billionaire Crypto Fortunes Swing So Much

Crypto fortunes rise and fall with the market far faster than fortunes built on traditional businesses. Forbes calculated in February 2026 that the ten biggest crypto billionaires had lost more than $60 billion combined since October 2025. Saylor, Armstrong and the Winklevoss twins all lost billions in a few months without doing anything differently.

Private company valuations add a second layer of uncertainty. The Bloomberg Billionaires Index put CZ at $45.1 billion in a February 2026 snapshot, while Forbes valued him at $110 billion only weeks later. Neither is wrong; they simply value Binance differently. The same is true for Tether, where a hypothetical $500 billion valuation would more than double Devasini's estimated wealth overnight.

## What Their Bets Mean for You

It is tempting to copy the richest people in crypto, but their positions are rarely comparable to yours. Most crypto-made billionaires own businesses, not just coins, and many of the investors hold crypto as 1% to 5% of a fortune that would survive it going to zero. Dalio's personal 1% allocation is a far better model for most people than Saylor's all-in bet.

What you can take from them is the pattern behind the winners. Saylor, the Winklevoss twins and Durov all bought steadily and held through brutal crashes, and none of them needed to sell at the bottom. If you want to build a position the same way, **[dollar-cost averaging](/posts/what-is-dollar-cost-averaging)** lets you buy small fixed amounts over time without trying to time the market, which is much closer to how patient holders actually got rich.

## Conclusion

The billionaires who own crypto range from CZ, whose estimated $110 billion fortune comes almost entirely from Binance, to Elon Musk and Peter Thiel, for whom crypto is a small side bet. The biggest crypto fortunes were made by owning exchanges and stablecoin issuers, while early holders like Saylor and the Winklevoss twins are the exception rather than the rule.

Treat every net worth figure as a snapshot with a date, not a fact. Before you put your own money in, decide how much you could afford to lose, start small, and learn the basics of storing your coins safely, because none of these fortunes would have survived a lost private key.
