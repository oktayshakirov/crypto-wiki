Gemini is a New York cryptocurrency exchange and custodian that operates as a chartered trust company rather than as a conventional money transmitter. That single legal choice, made when Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss founded the firm in 2014, explains most of what the platform is and is not good at. It is why Gemini can hold client assets in a fiduciary capacity, why it lists far fewer tokens than offshore rivals, why institutions route custody through it, and why its retail trading fees sit well above the market. Since September 12, 2025 its parent company has also been a Nasdaq-listed public company, which added quarterly financial disclosure to the picture.

## Why the Trust Charter Is the Whole Story

Most US exchanges operate as money services businesses registered with FinCEN plus a patchwork of state money transmitter licences. Gemini Trust Company, LLC instead holds a limited purpose trust charter from the New York State Department of Financial Services. The distinction is not cosmetic.

A trust charter is a banking-style authorisation. It brings NYDFS capital and liquidity requirements, periodic examinations by state bank examiners, and mandated consumer protection and cybersecurity programmes under the department's Part 500 rules. It also permits Gemini to act as a fiduciary, holding customer property in segregated accounts that are legally distinct from the company's own balance sheet.

What the charter does not do is make anything insured. Assets on Gemini carry neither FDIC nor SIPC protection, a point worth internalising before treating the platform like a bank. The charter reduces the chance of the operator misusing funds. It does nothing about market risk, and as the Earn episode showed, it does not reach products whose risk sits with a third party.

The charter also shapes the catalogue. Listing an asset at a chartered trust company means clearing an internal review designed to satisfy a state banking regulator, which is why Gemini offered roughly 99 cryptocurrencies across about 655 trading pairs as of 2026 while offshore venues list thousands. If you want obscure tokens, this is the wrong venue by design.

## What Gemini Lets You Trade

The product set has widened considerably from the plain spot exchange of the late 2010s. Alongside spot **[crypto trading](/posts/crypto-trading-vs-holding)** in majors like **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** and **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)** plus a curated set of **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)**, Gemini now offers US equities, prediction contracts, margin and **[staking](/posts/crypto-staking)**, moving it toward a general brokerage rather than a crypto-only venue.

Three interfaces sit on top of that. The retail app handles quick buys, recurring purchases and price alerts, and suits **[crypto beginners](/posts/crypto-for-beginners)** who value simplicity over cost. ActiveTrader exposes a full order book, depth charts and advanced order types. REST, WebSocket and FIX APIs serve algorithmic traders and institutions.

Gemini Custody is the piece that genuinely has few peers. Offered under the trust charter, it holds assets offline in segregated accounts with account-level reporting and role-based access controls, aimed at funds and corporate treasuries rather than retail users. The IPO prospectus put platform assets above USD 21 billion and institutional clients at roughly 10,000 as of September 2025.

## The Earn Collapse and a 232 Percent Recovery

Gemini Earn let users lend crypto for yield. The lending was not done by Gemini. It was done by Genesis Global Capital, and when Genesis halted withdrawals in November 2022 during the contagion following the FTX failure, roughly 29,000 Earn users found their assets frozen. Around USD 1.1 billion had been placed through the programme by New Yorkers alone.

The resolution took 18 months and ran through the Genesis bankruptcy and a settlement with the New York Attorney General. In May 2024 Earn users received USD 2.18 billion in digital assets back in kind, with about 97 percent distributed immediately and the remainder inside 12 months. Because the return was made in crypto rather than at November 2022 dollar values, and because prices had risen sharply in the interim, Gemini characterised the outcome as a 232 percent recovery against the value at the moment withdrawals stopped.

That is an unusually complete result by the standards of crypto insolvencies, and it is the single most informative episode in the company's history. It also carries a lesson the marketing does not: the charter governed Gemini's own custody, not Genesis's book. Any yield product that routes your assets to a counterparty imports that counterparty's balance sheet, whatever the front end's licence says. Understanding **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** yourself is the only structural answer to that class of risk.

## Life as a Public Company

Gemini Space Station, Inc. priced its initial public offering at USD 28 per share on September 11, 2025, raising about USD 425 million and valuing the company at roughly USD 3.3 billion before trading opened. The stock began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under GEMI on September 12, opened at USD 37.01 and closed its first session up about 14 percent. Nasdaq itself invested USD 50 million around the listing.

For users, the listing matters less as a vote of confidence than as a disclosure mechanism. A public Gemini files quarterly and annual reports, which means revenue mix, trading volumes, custody balances and legal contingencies become matters of record rather than press releases. If you are weighing counterparty risk on a custodial venue, audited public filings are more useful than any trust badge.

## The Fee Problem

This is where Gemini is hardest to defend, and where most reviews are too polite.

The ActiveTrader schedule dated July 9, 2026 opens at 0.600 percent maker and 1.200 percent taker. That entry taker rate is roughly an order of magnitude above what large competitors charge new users. Costs fall to 0.400 percent maker and 0.800 percent taker once you clear USD 10,000 in 30-day volume or hold USD 1 million in eligible assets, and the schedule bottoms out at 0 percent maker and 0.02 percent taker, but only in the USD 250 million volume tier that no retail account will ever see.

The retail app is worse still, layering a flat convenience fee on top of a spread. ACH deposits are typically free and wires incur bank charges, while on-chain withdrawals reflect network costs plus policy fees.

The practical consequence: if you trade with any frequency and are not moving institutional size, Gemini will cost you materially more than the alternatives, and that drag compounds against you regardless of how well you read **[crypto volatility](/posts/crypto-volatility)**. The charter and the custody are what you are paying for. Decide whether you need them.

## What to Weigh Before You Sign Up

- **Price the fees against your actual volume.** Run your expected monthly turnover through the ActiveTrader tiers before opening an account. At small size the entry tier is punishing, and no amount of regulatory comfort offsets a 1.2 percent taker fee on frequent trades.
- **Separate the charter from the product.** The trust charter protects Gemini's custody of your assets. It said nothing about Genesis, and it will say nothing about the next third party behind a yield or lending product. Read what sits behind any programme offering a return.
- **Nothing here is insured.** No FDIC, no SIPC. Insurance covers certain hot wallet security events within policy limits, not price moves, not your own account compromise.
- **Expect a narrow catalogue.** Roughly 99 assets in 2026 is deliberate. If your thesis needs small caps, you will be using another venue alongside this one.
- **Harden the account yourself.** Enable hardware-key two-factor authentication rather than SMS, switch on withdrawal address allowlisting, and treat unsolicited contact as hostile. Platform controls do not stop **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)** aimed at you directly.
- **Keep only working capital on-platform.** Use the exchange to trade and a hardware wallet to hold, and track the split with whatever you use to **[monitor your portfolio](/posts/how-to-track-your-crypto-portfolio)**.

## Who Gemini Actually Suits

Gemini fits institutions, funds, corporate treasuries and high-net-worth individuals who need a US-chartered custodian and will pay for the legal structure. For that audience the trust charter, the segregated cold storage, the SOC 2 audits and now the public filings form a coherent package that very few competitors can match on paper.

It fits active retail traders considerably less well. The fee schedule is the reason, and it is not a small penalty. A cost-sensitive trader who wants regulated US access will find cheaper routes to the same majors, and a trader who wants breadth will not find it here at all.

The honest summary is that Gemini sells regulatory structure and custody quality at a premium, and has become more transparent about its own finances since the 2025 listing. Whether that premium is worth paying depends entirely on whether you are the kind of holder for whom a chartered fiduciary matters. Understanding the underlying **[blockchain](/posts/what-is-blockchain)** mechanics and the difference between custodial and self-custodied holdings will tell you more about your real risk than the licence on the door.
