## Bullish: The NYSE-Listed Crypto Exchange Built for Institutions

Bullish is a regulated **[cryptocurrency exchange](/posts/understanding-crypto-exchanges)** for institutions and advanced traders, listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker BLSH since August 2025. It was founded in 2020 in Hong Kong by Brendan Blumer, the co-founder of Block.one, and it is led by chief executive Tom Farley, a former president of the NYSE Group. The same company owns CoinDesk, one of the best-known names in crypto media, data and indices.

Bullish started with an unusually large balance sheet. Block.one seeded it with 164,000 bitcoin, worth about 9.7 billion US dollars at the time, plus 100 million dollars in cash and 20 million EOS tokens, and a separate 300 million dollar round brought in backers including Peter Thiel, Alan Howard, Louis Bacon and Richard Li. Trading opened to institutional clients in November 2021.

Where most exchanges compete on token listings and retail promotions, Bullish competes on regulation, deep liquidity in the majors, and the kind of corporate transparency that comes with a stock-market listing. That makes it a different proposition from retail-first platforms, and it is worth understanding before you open an account.

## A Short History: From Block.one to the NYSE

The road to a public listing took four years. In July 2021 Bullish agreed to go public through a merger with Far Peak, a special purpose acquisition company led by Tom Farley, at a valuation of 9 billion US dollars. The deal could not get its registration statement declared effective in time, and both sides terminated it in December 2022. Farley later joined Bullish and became its CEO.

The company spent the next phase building out its business beyond trading. In November 2023 it bought CoinDesk from Digital Currency Group, reportedly for around 75 million dollars, and in 2024 it added the data provider CCData. Together they gave Bullish a subscription business in market data, media, events and benchmark indices that earns revenue whether trading volume is high or low.

The second listing attempt worked. Bullish priced its initial public offering in August 2025 at 37 dollars a share, above an already raised range, selling 30 million shares to raise about 1.11 billion dollars. The stock closed its first day at 68 dollars. In an unusual move, part of the proceeds were taken in stablecoins rather than cash. The shine has since come off: the shares traded around 29 dollars on 21 October 2026, below the IPO price.

## Core Trading Platform and Services

Bullish is built around one design choice. Its central limit order book is combined with a deterministic automated market maker, so liquidity provided through the AMM sits directly in the order book alongside conventional limit orders. The company says this keeps liquidity stable even in volatile markets, and it is the main technical feature that separates Bullish from a standard exchange.

- **Spot Trading:** The core market, focused on **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)**, **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)** and other large assets rather than a long tail of small tokens. Bullish says it ranks among the top ten exchanges for bitcoin and ether volume.
- **Derivatives:** **[Perpetual futures](/posts/what-are-perpetual-futures)** launched in December 2023 after approval from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission. The derivatives venue now lists more than 40 perpetual contracts, including one on the CoinDesk 20 index, plus bitcoin options with daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly expiries.
- **Margin Trading:** Available for eligible clients alongside spot and derivatives, with the usual risk of forced liquidation when collateral runs short.
- **Stablecoin Markets:** Bullish leans into regulated **[stablecoins](/posts/what-are-stablecoins)** and became the first exchange to list SoFi USD, the stablecoin issued by the US bank SoFi.
- **Tokenized Securities:** In August 2026 Bullish began trading tokenized versions of its own BLSH shares on its regulated venue, its first trades of a tokenized security.
- **Data and Indices:** Through CoinDesk, Bullish runs benchmark indices that sit behind exchange-traded products from firms such as Morgan Stanley and Grayscale, part of the wider growth of **[crypto ETFs](/posts/crypto-etfs-explained)**.

By October 2025 the exchange reported more than 1.5 trillion US dollars in cumulative trading volume since its 2021 launch. Monthly volume swings with the market, from 50.9 billion dollars in June 2026 to 30.7 billion dollars in July 2026, according to the company's own monthly figures.

## The Equiniti Deal and the Tokenization Bet

The biggest move in Bullish's history so far is not a trading product. On 5 May 2026 it agreed to buy Equiniti, a transfer agent that keeps share registers for listed companies, from the private equity firm Siris. The deal is valued at 4.2 billion US dollars, made up of about 1.85 billion dollars in assumed debt and 2.35 billion dollars in Bullish stock.

Equiniti serves nearly 3,000 issuers and more than 20 million shareholders. The plan is to pair that regulated registry business with blockchain rails, so companies can issue **[tokenized stocks](/posts/what-are-tokenized-stocks)** that are recorded by a familiar, supervised transfer agent and can then trade on Bullish outside the United States. It is one of the most direct attempts yet to bring **[real-world asset tokenization](/posts/real-world-asset-tokenization)** into the plumbing of public equity markets. Management expected the deal to close in January 2027, and said in August 2026 that issuer-sponsored tokenized shares should start contributing to trading in 2027 rather than 2026.

## Security Measures

Bullish runs a full-reserve exchange: client assets are held on a one-to-one basis and kept segregated from company assets. For an exchange that holds your funds, that is the single most important safeguard, and it is the same principle whose absence brought down FTX.

As a company listed in New York, Bullish publishes audited financial statements and quarterly results, which gives outside investors and clients a level of visibility that most offshore exchanges do not offer. Regulators in four jurisdictions supervise different parts of the business, which adds capital, custody and reporting requirements on top.

Two points of history are worth knowing. Bullish's parent Block.one settled with the US Securities and Exchange Commission in 2019, paying 24 million dollars over its unregistered EOS token sale, and the founding team carries that association. And Bullish holds a large treasury of digital assets, so its reported profit or loss can swing hard with crypto prices: it reported a net loss of 604.9 million dollars in the first quarter of 2026 while its adjusted revenue was 92.8 million dollars. As of October 2026 we found no publicly reported hack of the exchange.

Because Bullish is custodial, you do not hold the private keys while your coins sit on the platform. Long-term holdings are better kept under your own control, and it is worth learning **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** safely before moving larger amounts.

## User Experience

Bullish is designed for professional traders first. The platform offers high-throughput APIs for programmatic strategies, connectivity through market access providers such as Paradigm, and the depth that market makers, hedge funds and proprietary trading firms look for. Its first named US clients at launch included the asset manager Nonco and the custodian BitGo.

Individuals are not left out entirely. Bullish publishes mobile apps on iOS and Android, and its US launch opened accounts to advanced individual traders for the first time. Still, the experience is closer to a professional terminal than to a beginner's app. There are fewer coins than on retail-first exchanges, and the educational material is mostly delivered through CoinDesk's newsroom rather than built into the exchange.

For traders who want a large, regulated venue with a simpler retail interface, **[Kraken](/exchanges/kraken)** and **[Coinbase](/exchanges/coinbase)** are the closer comparisons. For options specialists, **[Deribit](/exchanges/deribit)** remains the deeper market.

## Regulatory Compliance

Regulation is the core of the Bullish pitch, and it has built a licence portfolio across four regions:

- **Gibraltar:** The original home of the exchange, licensed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission as a distributed ledger technology provider since 2021. Its derivatives business also runs under GFSC approval.
- **Germany and the EU:** Bullish received custody, proprietary trading and brokerage licences from BaFin on 20 December 2024. On 5 September 2025 its German entity, Bullish Europe GmbH, was authorised under **[MiCA](/posts/what-is-mica)**, letting it serve clients across the European Union from its Frankfurt base.
- **United States:** New York's financial regulator granted a BitLicense and a money transmitter licence on 17 September 2025. US spot trading launched on 1 October 2025 in 20 states and territories, including New York, California, Florida and Washington, D.C., with a waitlist elsewhere.
- **Hong Kong:** Bullish also holds licences in Hong Kong, the city where it was founded.

US derivatives are the gap. In its August 2026 earnings call, management said it expected to offer US clients perpetuals, dated futures and options within several months, roughly a year earlier than first planned, through its overseas platform and an approved futures commission merchant. That depends on approvals that had not been announced as of October 2026.

## Points to Consider

- **Built for Professionals:** The product, the asset list and the support model are aimed at institutions and active traders. Beginners will find simpler on-ramps elsewhere.
- **Limited US Coverage:** US access covers spot trading in 20 states and territories as of October 2025, and US derivatives were still pending approval in October 2026.
- **Custodial Risk:** Full reserves and regulation reduce counterparty risk but do not remove it. Coins on any exchange are not under your own keys.
- **Fees Vary by Account:** Promotional zero-fee offers and institutional tiers differ by region and product. Read the current fee schedule on the official site rather than relying on launch-day headlines.
- **Corporate Bets:** The Equiniti deal turns Bullish into a much bigger, more traditional financial business. It could pay off, but it also adds debt and integration risk to the company behind the exchange.

## Conclusion

Bullish is the crypto exchange that most closely resembles a traditional market operator. It has an NYSE listing, a former NYSE president as CEO, a full-reserve custody model, licences in Gibraltar, the EU, New York and Hong Kong, and a data and index business through CoinDesk that keeps revenue coming when trading slows. Its order book with a built-in market maker gives it real depth in the major assets, and the Equiniti deal is a serious bet that tokenized shares will become part of mainstream equity markets.

The trade-offs are just as clear. Bullish offers fewer coins than retail-first exchanges, its US offering is limited to spot in part of the country, and its share price has fallen below the IPO level as crypto trading cooled. For institutions, funds and experienced traders who value regulation and transparency above coin selection, Bullish is one of the strongest choices in the market. For a first-time buyer, a simpler retail platform is usually the better place to start.
