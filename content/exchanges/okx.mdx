OKX is one of the largest cryptocurrency exchanges in the world, founded in 2017 as OKEx by Star Xu, registered in the Seychelles and operating across spot, derivatives, earn products and a self-custody Web3 wallet. It is also, since February 2025, a convicted criminal enterprise in the United States: its operating entity pleaded guilty to running an unlicensed money transmitting business and paid USD 505 million. That fact belongs near the top of any honest assessment.

## The February 2025 Guilty Plea

On February 24, 2025, Aux Cayes FinTech Co, the Seychelles entity that operates OKX, pleaded guilty in a US court to one count of operating an unlicensed money transmitting business.

The admitted conduct is specific. From 2018 through early 2024, OKX contravened its own stated policy prohibiting US users from accessing its platform. Over that period the platform was used to facilitate more than USD 5 billion in suspicious transactions and criminal proceeds.

The penalty came to nearly USD 505 million: an USD 84.4 million criminal fine plus USD 420.3 million in forfeited fees. The settlement also requires OKX to retain an external compliance consultant, hired in early 2024, through February 2027.

OKX's own characterisation was that legacy compliance gaps had allowed certain US customers onto its global platform, that they represented a small percentage of its total user base, and that none remain. That framing is worth weighing against the admitted facts: a six-year period, five billion dollars in illicit flow, and a guilty plea rather than a negotiated civil settlement without admission.

For a user, the practical reading is not that OKX is uniquely dangerous. It is that the exchange's compliance function was, by its own admission, non-functional for six years, and that the remediation is externally supervised until 2027 rather than complete. An exchange under a monitorship is arguably better scrutinised than one that has never been examined, and it got there by being caught.

## What the Platform Actually Offers

Setting the legal history aside, OKX is a genuinely capable venue with deep liquidity.

Spot trading covers **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)**, **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)** and a very wide set of **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)**. The derivatives business is among the largest anywhere, with perpetual swaps, dated futures and options, and liquidity that makes it a serious venue for professional flow rather than only retail speculation. Order types, APIs and margin modes are comprehensive.

OKX Earn aggregates **[staking](/posts/crypto-staking)**, savings and structured products, and the underlying **[proof-of-stake](/posts/what-is-proof-of-stake)** yields are real while the structured products are not deposits and carry principal risk that the presentation tends to understate.

Fees follow a tiered maker-taker schedule with discounts driven by volume and OKB holdings, and they are competitive at the top tiers and unremarkable at the bottom.

## The Web3 Wallet Is the Interesting Part

OKX's most distinctive product is not the exchange. It is the Web3 Wallet, a genuine self-custody wallet integrated into the same application as the custodial exchange account.

That integration is unusual and useful. You can hold assets in a wallet whose keys you control, reach **[DeFi](/posts/what-is-defi)** protocols, NFT markets and multiple chains, and move between that and the exchange without exporting a seed phrase into a separate app. For a user who wants to graduate from custodial holding to self-custody, the friction that usually prevents it is largely removed.

It also blurs a distinction that matters. Assets in the exchange account are OKX's liability to you; assets in the Web3 Wallet are yours, with no recourse if you lose the keys. The interface makes those feel like two tabs of one balance, and they are legally and practically nothing alike. Anyone using both should be certain at all times which one they are in, and understanding **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** is what makes that distinction real rather than theoretical.

## Proof of Reserves, and the Exchange Token

OKX publishes regular proof-of-reserves attestations using Merkle tree verification, which lets an individual user confirm their own balance is included in the total the exchange claims to hold. Most client assets are held in cold storage.

The standard caveat applies with full force. A proof of reserves shows assets at a point in time. It does not disclose liabilities, does not establish that the assets are unencumbered, and does not bind the next day's balance. It is a meaningful improvement on nothing and considerably weaker than an audit.

OKB, the exchange token, provides fee discounts and tiered benefits. Like every exchange token it is a leveraged bet on the venue's trading volume rather than a diversifying asset, and it ties your holdings to the fortunes of the same counterparty holding your collateral.

The ecosystem also includes X Layer and previously OKC as chain infrastructure, and **[NFT](/posts/are-nfts-dead)** and **[GameFi](/posts/what-is-gamefi)** surfaces whose relevance has varied considerably with market conditions.

## Availability and Jurisdiction

OKX restricts a number of jurisdictions and, following the DOJ case, operates a separate and considerably more limited platform for the United States rather than serving US users from the global venue. Product availability for derivatives, earn and wallet features differs markedly by region, and the entity you contract with depends on where you are.

That fragmentation is the direct consequence of the 2025 plea. The global platform's historic willingness to accept users it said it excluded is exactly what was prosecuted, and the current structure exists to prevent a repeat.

## Practical Cautions

- **Read the guilty plea, not the press release.** Six years, five billion dollars in suspicious flow, an admission of criminal conduct and a monitor in place until February 2027. Weigh that as counterparty information.
- **Know which balance you are looking at.** The exchange account and the Web3 Wallet sit side by side and are opposite in kind. One is a claim on OKX; the other is yours alone.
- **Derivatives here are deep and unforgiving.** Liquidity is genuinely good, which makes it easy to take size that a small adverse move will close.
- **Earn is not saving.** Structured products carry principal risk. Read what generates the yield before accepting it.
- **Proof of reserves is a snapshot.** Verify your own leaf in the Merkle tree if you use it, and do not mistake the exercise for an audit.
- **Harden the account.** Hardware-key two-factor authentication, withdrawal allowlisting, and the assumption that unsolicited contact is hostile, because **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)** aimed at you personally are outside any platform's remedy.

## Where OKX Stands

OKX is a top-tier exchange by liquidity and product breadth, with the best-integrated self-custody wallet of any major venue and derivatives markets deep enough for professional use. On capability it competes directly with **[Binance](/exchanges/binance)**, and for many traders it is the better interface.

It is also an exchange whose operator admitted to six years of criminal non-compliance and paid half a billion dollars for it, and whose remediation is still under external supervision. Those two descriptions are both true and neither cancels the other. A large, liquid, capable venue that was run badly enough to warrant a guilty plea is a specific kind of counterparty, and the correct response is neither to dismiss it nor to ignore the record.

Keep trading capital here if the liquidity serves you, use the Web3 Wallet for what it is genuinely good at, and hold nothing long-term in the custodial account. **[Understanding crypto exchanges](/posts/understanding-crypto-exchanges)** means being able to hold a venue's capability and its conduct history in view at the same time.
