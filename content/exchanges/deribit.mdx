Deribit is a crypto derivatives exchange that trades options, futures and perpetual swaps, and it has held the deepest Bitcoin and Ethereum options order book in the market for most of its existence. Founded in the Netherlands in 2016 by John Jansen and Marius Jansen, it was built for market makers, funds and volatility desks rather than retail buyers, and it has never offered a meaningful spot business. Since Coinbase completed its USD 2.9 billion acquisition on August 14, 2025, Deribit has been the derivatives engine inside a US-listed public company, a change that is now reshaping both venues.

## The Options Book Is the Moat

Almost every crypto exchange lists perpetual futures. Very few have ever built a functioning options market, because options require liquidity spread across a grid of strikes and expiries rather than concentrated in a single contract. Liquidity begets liquidity on that grid, and Deribit won it early.

The resulting share is extreme. Deribit still accounted for roughly 85 percent of crypto options open interest in 2026, and within the combined Coinbase derivatives complex it held between USD 27.4 billion and USD 27.8 billion of the nearly USD 28.9 billion in total open interest, around 95 percent of the group. Coinbase Derivatives reported USD 4.75 billion in daily volume across the integrated platforms following the merger.

That dominance is what professional traders actually come for. A vol desk needs to buy a specific strike at a specific expiry in size without moving the price, and outside Deribit the crypto options market has historically not been able to accommodate that.

## Where the Moat Is Starting to Leak

The interesting development of 2026 is that Deribit's monopoly on scale is no longer unchallenged, and the competition is not another crypto exchange.

In April 2026, open interest in options on BlackRock's IBIT spot Bitcoin ETF reached USD 27.61 billion, overtaking Deribit's USD 26.9 billion and marking the first time a regulated US venue led offshore Bitcoin options open interest. Institutions that want Bitcoin volatility exposure inside a familiar regulatory wrapper can now get it without opening an offshore derivatives account at all.

This matters for anyone assessing Deribit's durability. Its structural advantage was that no regulated alternative existed at size. That is no longer true for Bitcoin, and the gap is being closed by listed products rather than by rival crypto venues.

## What You Can Trade and How the Margin Works

The product set is narrow by design: options, dated futures and perpetual swaps on **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** and **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**, with a rotating set of large-cap **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)** as demand and liquidity allow.

- **Options.** European-style, cash-settled, with strikes and expiries running from daily to quarterly and beyond. Settlement is in the underlying crypto rather than dollars, which changes the payoff arithmetic in ways newcomers routinely misjudge.
- **Perpetual swaps.** Funding-rate contracts with no expiry. If funding, premiums and mark price are not yet second nature, start with **[what are perpetual futures](/posts/what-are-perpetual-futures)** before putting capital at risk.
- **Portfolio margin.** The feature that keeps professional flow on the platform. Offsetting positions are margined as a book rather than individually, so a hedged structure ties up far less collateral than it would on a venue that margins each leg in isolation.
- **Block trades and RFQ.** Large orders are negotiated off the order book and printed to it, avoiding the slippage that a size order would otherwise cause.

## Custody, and the 2022 Hot Wallet Loss

Deribit is fully custodial, so your collateral sits with the exchange and you carry its counterparty risk. The majority of client assets are held in cold storage, with proof-of-reserves data published for verification.

The clearest test of that arrangement came in November 2022, when attackers drained roughly USD 28 million from a Deribit hot wallet. The company covered the shortfall from its own reserves and no client balance was affected. That is the outcome you want from an operator, and it is worth weighing against the many venues that have socialised such losses onto users.

It does not change the underlying structure. Collateral on a derivatives exchange is working capital, not storage, and anything you are not actively trading against belongs elsewhere. If that separation is not yet part of your routine, **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** covers the mechanics.

## Being Absorbed Into Coinbase

The acquisition has moved well past the paperwork stage, and the integration is now the main thing changing about this exchange.

As of October 1, 2026, perpetuals trading that previously ran on Coinbase International Exchange is handled through Deribit by Coinbase, consolidating the group's derivatives flow onto Deribit's infrastructure. In May 2026, Coinbase Financial Markets opened a route for US institutional clients to trade global crypto options and perpetual futures through a CFTC-regulated futures commission merchant with direct Deribit access. Coinbase has said options will appear directly in its main app for eligible users in selected jurisdictions from late October 2026.

The practical effect is that **[Coinbase](/exchanges/coinbase)** is becoming the retail-facing surface and Deribit the matching engine underneath. For existing Deribit users this has so far meant continuity rather than disruption, but the venue's independence is steadily dissolving, and product decisions now answer to a public company's disclosure and compliance posture.

## Who Can Actually Open an Account

Deribit does not accept US retail residents, and it restricts a number of other jurisdictions based on local law. The May 2026 institutional route is exactly that: a path for qualified US institutions through a regulated intermediary, not an opening for individual American traders.

Its Dubai entity, Deribit FZE, holds a Virtual Asset Service Provider licence from Dubai's Virtual Asset Regulatory Authority, granted conditionally in April 2024 and among the first issued to a derivatives exchange there. The company operated from Panama for years before relocating, a history worth knowing when assessing how long its current regulatory footing has existed.

The sign-up flow remains the only authoritative answer on eligibility, and it changes. Traders comparing venues for perps liquidity commonly look at **[OKX](/exchanges/okx)** alongside it, though nothing matches the options depth.

## Risks Specific to This Venue

- **This is not a beginner venue, and the platform does not pretend otherwise.** Options pricing, the Greeks, funding mechanics and liquidation logic all have to be understood before the first trade, not after it. **[Understanding crypto exchanges](/posts/understanding-crypto-exchanges)** is the prerequisite, not the destination.
- **Crypto-settled options behave differently.** Because contracts settle in BTC or ETH rather than dollars, your payoff is denominated in an asset whose price is itself moving. The hedge you think you have may not be the hedge you have.
- **Leverage liquidates faster than most expect.** Deribit's risk engine will close positions on maintenance margin breaches without regard for your conviction. Position sizing is the entire game.
- **Counterparty risk is real even with a clean record.** Cold storage, proof of reserves and a covered 2022 loss are good signs, not guarantees. Keep collateral to what the strategy requires.
- **Ownership now shapes the roadmap.** Coinbase decides what this venue becomes. Expect continued consolidation of the two platforms, and read announcements about product migration as material to your access.
- **Watch the regulated alternatives.** If IBIT options or CME products can express your view inside a wrapper your mandate already permits, the offshore account may not be worth the operational overhead.

## The Verdict for 2026

Deribit remains the default venue for anyone whose strategy genuinely requires crypto options liquidity at size: volatility funds, market makers, prop desks, structured product issuers and the small population of individual traders who trade vol rather than direction. On options depth across the strike and expiry grid it has no real peer, and portfolio margining makes complex books capital-efficient in a way single-contract margining cannot.

It suits almost nobody else. There is no retail spot experience, no on-ramp for a first crypto purchase, and no reason for a directional trader to be here rather than somewhere simpler. Anyone still learning the basics is better served by **[crypto for beginners](/posts/crypto-for-beginners)** and a plan for **[trading versus holding](/posts/crypto-trading-vs-holding)** than by a professional derivatives book.

The live question for 2026 and beyond is not whether Deribit is good at what it does. It is whether an offshore specialist retains its advantage now that it sits inside Coinbase and regulated US products have started matching its open interest. For the moment the answer is yes on options depth, and increasingly no on everything else.
