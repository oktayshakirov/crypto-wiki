MEXC is a centralized cryptocurrency exchange founded in 2018 and registered in the Seychelles, built around two aggressive propositions: an enormous altcoin catalogue and pricing that removes trading fees almost entirely. It charges 0 percent maker fees on spot and 0 percent maker with 0.02 percent taker on futures, where leverage reaches 200x. It also has a documented pattern of freezing customer accounts under its risk-control system, and that is the part of its record a prospective user most needs to read.

## The Account Freeze Problem

This is not a rumour or a scattering of complaints on a forum. It became a public incident that the exchange's own leadership apologised for.

A trader known as The White Whale had roughly USD 3.15 million frozen in July 2025 under MEXC's risk control provisions. The case went public, on-chain investigator ZachXBT amplified it, and the exchange reversed course: in late October 2025 MEXC's chief strategy officer issued a public apology, the full amount was returned in early November, and the company promised a fast-track appeals channel for frozen accounts.

The individual outcome was correct. The systemic picture is what should concern a reader. The White Whale stated they had been contacted by hundreds of other traders describing indefinite freezes with no access to funds, and users have continued to report withdrawal difficulties on social media.

Understand the mechanism, because it is the point. Risk-control provisions in an exchange's terms of service typically permit it to suspend an account on suspicion of market manipulation, abnormal trading patterns or bonus abuse, at its own discretion, without a defined timeline and without an external appeal. At a licensed venue a customer has a regulator to escalate to. At an unlicensed Seychelles entity there is no such body, which means the exchange is simultaneously the party holding your funds, the party alleging misconduct, and the only available adjudicator.

What resolved the USD 3.15 million case was not a process. It was publicity, applied by a well-known investigator on behalf of a trader with a large enough balance to attract attention. Most users have neither.

## Zero Fees Are Not Free

MEXC's pricing genuinely is among the lowest anywhere: 0 percent maker on spot markets, and 0 percent maker with a 0.02 percent taker fee on futures.

An exchange cannot run on nothing, so it is worth being clear about where the revenue comes from. Futures taker fees at scale, liquidation flow from 200x leverage positions, listing arrangements with projects wanting access to the catalogue, and spread on the long tail all contribute. None of that is improper, and it does mean the business model is oriented toward high-frequency leveraged activity rather than toward buy-and-hold users paying commissions.

The cost you actually bear on small-cap pairs is slippage, not commission. A zero maker fee on a book too thin to absorb your order is worse than a 0.1 percent fee on a liquid one, and only the order book at your intended size will tell you which you are facing.

## 200x Leverage, Stated Plainly

At 200x, a move of roughly half a percent against your position eliminates it. **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** does that routinely within a single candle, and **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)** and the smaller assets move more.

There is no strategy at that multiple. Position survival is determined by ordinary market noise rather than by any thesis, and the liquidation engine closes you out without regard to whether your direction was right. Extreme leverage exists on a venue because liquidations generate revenue, and its prominence in the marketing is information about which users the platform expects to profit from.

## Breadth Without Vetting

The catalogue is very large, extending far past **[DeFi](/posts/what-is-defi)** majors into tokens in their earliest days. For a trader hunting early listings that is the entire appeal.

It also guarantees exposure to assets that will fail, and some that were constructed to. The exchange performs no diligence a user could rely on, and listing carries no information about a project's quality. **[Do your own research](/posts/do-your-own-research)** is not a slogan on a platform like this; it is the only filter in operation.

MEXC also runs launchpad and airdrop programmes, **[staking](/posts/crypto-staking)** and earn products, and the MX token for fee discounts and tiered benefits.

## Regulation, and Why It Matters Here Specifically

MEXC is registered in the Seychelles, restricts US residents and several other jurisdictions, and holds no authorisation comparable to a MiCA-licensed European exchange or a US-regulated venue. There is no deposit protection and no supervisory authority available to a customer.

On most offshore exchanges that absence is a background risk. Here it is the specific mechanism through which the freeze problem operates. An exchange that can suspend your account at its discretion, with no defined appeal window and no regulator above it, is one where the terms of service are the entire extent of your protection. Reading those terms before depositing is not excessive caution on this platform; it is the minimum.

## Practical Cautions

- **Assume a freeze is possible and size accordingly.** Keep only what you are actively trading on the platform, and withdraw profits promptly rather than accumulating a balance that becomes worth freezing.
- **Withdraw in normal increments.** Sudden large or unusual movements are exactly what risk-control systems flag. This is defensive behaviour, not evasion.
- **Publicity resolved the known case, not process.** The promised fast-track appeals channel is new and unproven. Do not rely on it.
- **Zero fees redirect the cost rather than removing it.** Slippage on thin books and liquidation flow on leverage are where the revenue is.
- **200x is a warning, not a tool.** Half a percent ends the position. Its availability tells you what the venue optimises for.
- **Most early listings fail.** Access is the product; validation is not included.
- **Self-custody is the answer.** **[How to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** in your own wallet is the only measure that addresses both custodial failure and discretionary account suspension, and **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)** aimed at your credentials have no platform remedy either way.

## Who MEXC Suits

MEXC suits experienced traders who specifically want the earliest possible access to small-cap tokens at near-zero commission, who verify liquidity themselves, who keep balances small and withdraw regularly, and who have read and accepted that the exchange can suspend their account at its own discretion with no external appeal. Used that narrowly, it does a job that licensed venues will not do.

It suits nobody else, and the freeze record is the reason rather than the fees or the catalogue. A **[crypto beginner](/posts/crypto-for-beginners)** should not be here at all, and anyone considering **[trading versus holding](/posts/crypto-trading-vs-holding)** over any meaningful horizon should hold nothing on this platform. Traders wanting comparable breadth with a less troubling record on customer funds generally use **[KuCoin](/exchanges/kucoin)** or **[Bybit](/exchanges/bybit)**.

The summary a reader most needs: MEXC offers the cheapest fees and the broadest early access in the market, and it has frozen customer funds under discretionary risk controls, returned a high-profile case only after public pressure, and left an unknown number of smaller cases unresolved. **[Understanding crypto exchanges](/posts/understanding-crypto-exchanges)** means recognising that the ability to withdraw is a more fundamental feature than the fee schedule, and pricing it accordingly.
