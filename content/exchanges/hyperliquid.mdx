## Hyperliquid: An On-Chain Order Book Exchange for Perpetual Futures

Hyperliquid is a decentralized, non-custodial cryptocurrency exchange built around on-chain perpetual futures. It was founded by Jeff Yan, a Harvard graduate and former Hudson River Trading quant, together with a pseudonymous co-founder known as iliensinc. Hyperliquid Labs came together in 2022 and the exchange opened to traders in early 2023. Rather than a conventional company with a head office, Hyperliquid runs as a protocol on its own layer-1 blockchain, prioritizing speed, transparency, and self-custody across major assets like **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)**, **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**, and a widening set of **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)**.

Its core differentiator is a fully on-chain central limit order book (CLOB) rather than an automated market maker. That means professional order types, low-latency matching, and transparent state updates, while traders keep control of their own keys and collateral.

## Core Protocol: How Hyperliquid Works

Hyperliquid is purpose-built for derivatives. The protocol centers on perpetual futures with position-level risk management, funding-rate mechanics, and a native matching engine that settles on a chain the team wrote themselves.

- **Trading Model:** On-chain CLOB matching with full price-time priority, visible depth, and transparent settlement. Unlike AMMs such as **[Uniswap](/exchanges/uniswap)**, orders rest on a book instead of relying on pools and bonding curves.
- **Its Own Layer 1:** Hyperliquid does not rent blockspace from another network. It runs a purpose-built chain secured by a delegated proof-of-stake consensus called HyperBFT, which targets sub-second confirmations so that on-chain trading feels closer to a centralized venue.
- **HyperCore and HyperEVM:** The exchange logic lives in HyperCore, while HyperEVM is an Ethereum-compatible execution layer secured by the same validator set. That lets developers deploy familiar smart contracts alongside the order book instead of bridging out to a separate chain.
- **The HYPE Token:** HYPE is the network's native asset. It pays gas on HyperEVM, it is staked to secure consensus, and it carries governance rights over protocol parameters. It launched in November 2024 through a genesis airdrop of 310 million tokens, roughly 31 percent of the 1 billion capped supply, distributed to early users with no allocation to private investors.
- **Collateral and Margin:** Traders post stablecoin collateral to open and maintain positions, with isolated and cross-margin modes available. Liquidations trigger once maintenance thresholds are breached.
- **Risk Engine and Funding:** A continuous funding-rate system keeps perp prices anchored to their index. Leverage amplifies gains, but it magnifies losses just as quickly in volatile digital assets.
- **Ecosystem Context:** As part of **[DeFi](/posts/what-is-defi)**, Hyperliquid sits inside a wider on-chain stack of wallets, bridges, and analytics. If the space is new to you, the basics of **[blockchain](/posts/what-is-blockchain)** are worth covering first, and this primer on **[staking](/posts/crypto-staking)** explains the validator economics that secure the chain.

## Security Measures

As a decentralized exchange, Hyperliquid is non-custodial: you hold your keys and collateral through a compatible wallet at all times. That removes centralized custody risk, but it replaces it with a different set of exposures.

- **Smart-Contract and Protocol Risk:** Any protocol can carry logic or implementation bugs. Audits and continuous testing reduce that risk without eliminating it, so size positions against your own risk tolerance.
- **The JELLY Incident:** In March 2025 a trader deliberately manipulated the thinly traded JELLYJELLY perp, opening offsetting longs and shorts and then pushing the token's spot price up more than 400 percent within an hour. The protocol's liquidity vault, HLP, inherited the losing short and faced roughly 12 million dollars of unrealized losses. Validators voted within minutes to delist the market and settle positions at the opening price, and affected traders were reimbursed. It ended without user losses, but it exposed both the tail risk in low-liquidity perps and the reality that a small validator set can step into a market.
- **Oracle and Liquidation Mechanics:** Perp venues rely on index and mark prices to govern liquidations. Brief dislocations can trigger a liquidation even when spot recovers moments later, so keep leverage conservative.
- **Self-Custody Practices:** Because you control the keys, harden your setup. Hardware wallets and disciplined operational hygiene matter here, so review our guide on **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** and stay alert to phishing using the warning signs in **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)**.
- **Market Conditions:** Extreme sentiment drives both volatility and funding rates in perps markets. A gauge like the **[Fear and Greed Index](/tools/fear-and-greed-index)** adds context to risk taking, though it is not a trading signal on its own.

## User Experience

The interface is built for derivatives specialists but stays approachable once margin and funding click. Order tickets are comprehensive, positions and PnL update in real time, and charting covers the common indicators.

- **Onboarding:** Connect a wallet, deposit supported collateral, and you are trading. There is no centralized sign-up because the protocol is non-custodial. If venues and order flow are new to you, start with **[understanding crypto exchanges](/posts/understanding-crypto-exchanges)** and our **[crypto for beginners](/posts/crypto-for-beginners)** guide.
- **Performance and Latency:** Matching aims for near-instant feedback, which matters around funding prints, open interest shifts, and fast breakouts. The feel is deliberately closer to a centralized derivatives venue like **[Bybit](/exchanges/bybit)** than to a typical DEX.
- **Order Types and Controls:** Limit, market, post-only, reduce-only, scaled, and conditional stop orders are all standard, alongside position tools for managing complex exposure.
- **Fees and Funding:** Hyperliquid uses a maker and taker schedule with volume tiers, and you pay or receive funding depending on the long and short imbalance. Rates move with market conditions, so check current figures before sizing a strategy.
- **APIs and Automation:** A public API and an official Python SDK support programmatic trading, with an active ecosystem of community connectors and bots built on top of them.
- **Device Support:** The web app is primary, with a mobile app available. If you trade frequently, test your wallet and browser stack with a small amount before scaling up.

## Decentralization and the Regulatory Landscape

Hyperliquid is a protocol rather than a company-run exchange. It takes no custody of user funds and requires no KYC to interact with the contracts. Availability of the hosted front end does vary by jurisdiction, and several regions are geoblocked from the official interface even though the underlying chain stays permissionless. That gap between a restricted front end and an open protocol is a recurring theme across DeFi, and it is where most regulatory pressure currently lands.

Users remain responsible for following local law, including derivatives rules that in many countries restrict leveraged products for retail traders. Check whether your jurisdiction permits access before depositing, and take independent advice if you are unsure.

## Points to Consider

- **Who It Is For:** Hyperliquid suits experienced derivatives traders who want on-chain transparency and self-custody without giving up an order book. Pure spot buyers are usually better served by an AMM or a fiat on-ramp.
- **Leverage Discipline:** Perps amplify both directions. Conservative sizing, stops, and attention to funding matter more than precision on entries.
- **Fees and Costs:** Expect maker and taker fees plus variable funding, and budget for bridging costs when moving collateral onto the chain.
- **Liquidity Profile:** Blue-chip markets carry deep books and tight spreads, while tail assets can be thin and volatile. The JELLY episode is a reminder that low-liquidity perps behave very differently under stress.
- **Concentration Risk:** A small validator set and a lean core team deliver speed and decisiveness, but they also mean fewer hands on the emergency brake. Weigh that against the custody risk you avoid.

## Conclusion

Hyperliquid brought a professional order book fully on-chain, pairing the speed and control of a centralized derivatives venue with transparent settlement and self-custody. It rewards traders who already understand margin, funding, and liquidation dynamics, and it has grown into one of the most heavily used venues in on-chain derivatives without ever raising outside funding. Newer traders and long-term allocators may still prefer simpler spot rails and a patient view of **[the future of crypto](/posts/the-future-of-crypto)**.
