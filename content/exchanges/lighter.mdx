## Lighter: A Zero-Fee Perpetual Futures DEX Built on Its Own zk-Rollup

Lighter is a non-custodial **[decentralized exchange](/posts/what-is-a-dex)** for **[perpetual futures](/posts/what-are-perpetual-futures)** and spot trading that runs on an application-specific zero-knowledge rollup settled on **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**. It was founded by Vladimir Novakovski, a Harvard graduate and former Citadel engineer who had previously co-founded the networking app Lunchclub, and it is operated by Elliot Technologies, Inc., a US C-corporation. Public mainnet opened in October 2025 after roughly eight months of private testing.

Two things separate it from the rest of the on-chain derivatives field. Trading is free for retail users, with zero maker and taker fees. And the exchange proves its own behaviour: order matching in strict price-time priority, liquidations, funding and risk checks are all verified by custom zero-knowledge circuits rather than taken on trust. The team calls this the first exchange to offer verifiable matching and liquidations at performance comparable to a centralized venue.

## Core Protocol: How Lighter Works

Lighter is not deployed on a general-purpose **[layer-2 network](/posts/what-are-layer-2-blockchains)**. It is a dedicated zk-rollup whose only job is hosting a central limit order book and a perpetual margin system, which is what lets it target latency that feels closer to a centralized exchange.

- **Three-Part Architecture:** A sequencer orders transactions, builds blocks and posts oracle prices. A prover generates zk-SNARK proofs, using custom Plonky2 circuits, showing that every state transition followed the rules. Ethereum smart contracts hold collateral, verify those proofs and process deposits and withdrawals.
- **Verifiable Order Book:** Matching runs as a central limit order book with price-time priority, not an automated market maker. Because the matching itself is inside the proof, an operator cannot quietly reorder or front-run fills without the proof failing.
- **Zero Fees for Retail:** Makers and takers pay nothing. Revenue comes from premium market-maker tiers, liquidation fees, and a USDC revenue-share agreement with Circle announced in February 2026. **[Stablecoins](/posts/what-are-stablecoins)** serve as the collateral asset, so that arrangement is tied directly to how the exchange is used.
- **Escape Hatch:** If the sequencer censors a user for 14 days or more, a mechanism the docs call Desert Mode lets that user withdraw straight from the Ethereum contracts. It is the structural answer to the question of what happens when the operator misbehaves or disappears.
- **Market Coverage:** Perpetuals sit alongside spot markets, tokenized real-world assets and pre-market listings, spanning **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)**, ether and a long tail of **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)**.
- **Mobile and Tooling:** A mobile app launched in January 2026 for iOS and Android, and the company publishes open Python, Go and TypeScript SDKs plus its prover and contracts on GitHub for programmatic traders.

## Funding, Traction and the LIT Token

Lighter has raised USD 89 million across two rounds from Founders Fund, Ribbit Capital, Haun Ventures, Dragonfly, Craft Ventures and Robinhood Ventures, at a reported USD 1.5 billion valuation in November 2025. Roughly 80 percent of the engineering team came over from Lunchclub, with a mix of competitive programming alumni and former Citadel staff.

The traction is real rather than theoretical. Before its token generation event, Lighter processed USD 232 billion in 30-day volume and briefly out-traded both **[Hyperliquid](/exchanges/hyperliquid)** and **[Aster](/exchanges/aster)**. Once airdrop farming ended, activity settled to a more honest baseline: around USD 39 billion in 30-day perpetual volume, USD 487 million in total value locked and USD 766 million in open interest in early 2026, holding a stable top-five position among perpetual DEXs, on cumulative volume past USD 1.6 trillion.

LIT launched on 30 December 2025 with a fixed supply of 1 billion tokens and no inflation. A quarter of supply went out in the airdrop with no vesting, a quarter is reserved for future ecosystem programmes, and the remaining half is split between the team and investors under a one-year cliff with a multi-year unlock. The token is staked alongside liquidity pool deposits and unlocks fee tiers for larger accounts. Like most recent airdrops, the price fell sharply from its opening highs once unrestricted supply met real demand, which is worth keeping in mind before treating the token as a reason to trade there.

## Security Measures

Security on Lighter starts with the fact that you keep your keys and sign every order. That removes the counterparty risk that sank custodial venues, and replaces it with a different set of exposures.

- **Proof-Backed Execution:** The prover turns "trust our matching engine" into a verifiable claim. This is genuinely unusual and it is the platform's strongest security argument.
- **Audits and Open Code:** The contracts and prover are public, and audit reports are published in the project's security documentation. Audits reduce risk without eliminating it.
- **The October 2025 Incident:** A liquidation cascade briefly knocked the platform database offline shortly after mainnet launch. The team responded by restructuring the liquidity pool into strategies segregated by market type, so stress in one market is less able to spill into the rest.
- **Self-Custody Discipline:** Nothing here is recoverable by a support ticket. Use a hardware wallet where you can, manage token approvals actively, and read up on **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** before moving meaningful size.
- **Phishing Exposure:** New exchanges with a live airdrop narrative attract impersonators. Verify the domain each time and learn the usual patterns behind **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)**, because a signature on a malicious contract is final.

## User Experience

The interface is aimed at people who already trade derivatives. You connect a wallet, deposit USDC as collateral, and work a familiar order book with limit, market and trigger orders, live depth, funding rates and position-level risk data. There is no signup flow and no verification queue, so the distance between arriving and placing a first order is short.

The mobile app, launched in January 2026, carries the full market list rather than a stripped-down viewer, covering perpetuals, spot, tokenized real-world assets and pre-market listings. On the other end of the spectrum, the published SDKs and an agent kit for automated trading mean systematic users are not stuck with the web front end.

The main friction is the same one every rollup shares: moving collateral in and out touches Ethereum, so **[gas fees](/posts/what-are-gas-fees)** apply at the edges even though trading itself is free. Sizing deposits sensibly rather than shuttling funds back and forth is the practical fix.

## Decentralization and the Regulatory Landscape

Lighter occupies the awkward middle ground that most serious perpetual DEXs now share. The settlement layer is Ethereum and the contracts are permissionless, but the sequencer is operated by a company, and that company is a US C-corporation with named venture backers rather than an anonymous team. That makes it more accountable than a pseudonymous protocol and more exposed to regulators at the same time.

Perpetual futures remain restricted for retail users in several major jurisdictions, and front-end access can vary accordingly even when the underlying contracts stay open. Traders who want a licensed venue with fiat rails and a support desk are comparing a different kind of product entirely, and the trade-offs between the two models are worth understanding before choosing. ## Points to Consider

- **Zero Fees Are Not Zero Cost:** Funding payments on perpetual positions and Ethereum gas on deposits and withdrawals still apply, and on a leveraged position funding usually dwarfs what a fee schedule would have cost anyway.
- **Leverage Cuts Both Ways:** Perpetuals magnify losses exactly as efficiently as gains, and liquidation is automatic. The October 2025 cascade is a reminder that stress events are not hypothetical.
- **Sequencer Centralization:** A single operator sequences transactions today. The proofs constrain what it can do to your orders and the escape hatch limits how long it can ignore you, but this is not a decentralized validator set.
- **Token Performance:** LIT trades far below its opening level. Judge the exchange on its volume, depth and uptime rather than on the token chart.
- **Young Platform:** Mainnet is under a year old. Depth is strong in majors and thinner elsewhere, so test execution at your intended size before committing to it.

## Conclusion

Lighter is one of the more technically substantive entries in a crowded perpetual DEX field. The verifiable matching engine is a real answer to a real problem, since every other order book venue asks you to trust that fills were honest, and the zero-fee model paired with the Circle revenue share is a genuine attempt at a business that does not depend on taxing traders. The funding, the team background and a top-five position on volume all point the same way.

The caveats are the ordinary ones for a platform this young. The sequencer is centralized, mainnet has under a year of history, one stress event has already taken the database offline, and the token is well below its debut. For an experienced derivatives trader who is comfortable with self-custody and wants proof rather than promises about execution, Lighter is worth a serious look. For anyone newer to leveraged trading, the right order is to understand perpetuals and self-custody first, then decide where to trade them.
