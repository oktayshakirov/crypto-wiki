Paolo Ardoino is an Italian software engineer who runs Tether, the company behind USDt, the largest stablecoin in existence and one of the most systemically important private balance sheets in crypto. He joined Bitfinex as a developer in 2014, became Tether's chief technology officer and then its chief executive in December 2023, and has spent his tenure doing something his predecessors avoided: talking publicly and constantly about the company's reserves.

## From Genoa to the Centre of Crypto's Plumbing

Ardoino studied computer science at the University of Genoa and worked on trading systems in London before joining Bitfinex in 2014 as a senior software developer. He rose to chief technology officer of both Bitfinex and Tether, which were and are closely affiliated, and took over as Tether's CEO in December 2023.

He is an engineer by formation rather than a financier, and it shows in how he defends the company: in terms of system architecture, attack surfaces and throughput rather than the language of banking. That has been both an asset and a liability, because the central questions about Tether have always been accounting questions.

## What USDt Actually Is, and Why It Matters So Much

A **[stablecoin](/posts/what-are-stablecoins)** is a token designed to hold a fixed value, in USDt's case one US dollar, backed by reserves the issuer holds. USDt is not an investment product. It is settlement infrastructure.

Most crypto trading is denominated in it. When someone sells **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** on an offshore venue they are usually buying USDt, not dollars, because actual dollars require a bank and USDt does not. It moves across **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**, Tron, **[Solana](/posts/what-is-solana)** and a dozen other chains, settles in seconds, and works in countries where dollar bank accounts are difficult or impossible to obtain. In parts of Latin America, Africa, Turkey and Southeast Asia it functions as informal dollarisation.

As of April 2026 Tether reported more than USD 150 billion in circulating USDt against reserves above USD 185 billion. Ardoino has said the company expected roughly USD 15 billion in profit for 2025, generated largely from interest on reserves held in short-term US Treasuries, which makes Tether one of the most profitable companies per employee anywhere.

That profitability is the whole business model, and it is also the concentration risk. If a token backing a substantial fraction of global crypto settlement broke its peg, the consequences would not be contained to Tether's customers.

## The Reserve Question He Inherited

Tether's central controversy long predates Ardoino, and it is straightforward to state: for years the company asserted full backing while declining to produce a full independent audit.

In 2021 the New York Attorney General concluded that Tether's claims about its reserves had been misleading for certain periods and that the company had, at times, not held the backing it represented. Tether paid USD 18.5 million and agreed to stop serving New York customers. The CFTC brought a related action the same year. Those are findings of fact, not allegations, and they are the reason scepticism about the company has persisted regardless of subsequent improvements.

Ardoino's response has been a strategy of disclosure without a conventional audit. Tether publishes quarterly attestations from an accounting firm, has shifted the reserve composition heavily toward US Treasury bills and away from the commercial paper that drew the most criticism, and reports excess reserves above the amount needed to cover circulating tokens. Ardoino discusses the numbers openly and frequently, which is a genuine change in posture from the company's earlier silence.

What still does not exist is a full audit by a Big Four firm. An attestation is a point-in-time confirmation of balances presented to the accountant. An audit tests the underlying controls and the completeness of what was presented. The distinction matters, and it is the honest answer to whether Tether's backing is verified: it is attested, extensively and repeatedly, and it is not audited.

## The Move Back Into the United States

The regulatory landscape changed decisively in July 2025 with the passage of the GENIUS Act, which created a federal framework for dollar stablecoins, and Ardoino moved quickly.

Tether launched USAT, a separate dollar-backed token built to comply with the new US rules, describing it as a dollar token made in America and positioning it for institutions and regulated platforms rather than offshore trading. Ardoino has set a target of roughly USD 1 trillion for USAT within three to five years, which should be read as ambition rather than forecast.

The logic is that USDt and USAT serve different markets. USDt remains the offshore settlement asset, useful precisely because it operates outside the US banking perimeter. USAT is the compliant onshore product for counterparties that cannot touch the first one. Running both lets Tether keep its existing business while competing for regulated flow, and it is a sharper strategic read than the company's critics generally credit.

## The USD 500 Billion Question

In late 2025 Ardoino confirmed Tether was evaluating a private placement, reportedly seeking USD 15 billion to USD 20 billion for around a 3 percent stake, implying a valuation near USD 500 billion. That would place a stablecoin issuer alongside the largest technology companies on earth.

By April 2026 reporting indicated the raise was in trouble, with Tether setting a roughly two-week deadline and signalling it would postpone if too few investors committed. As of late September 2026 the round has not been confirmed as completed at that valuation, and the episode is a useful check on the narrative: extraordinary profitability has not automatically produced investor willingness to underwrite an extraordinary price for a company that still does not publish a full audit.

Ardoino has also directed capital well beyond stablecoins, into Bitcoin mining, artificial intelligence, communications and commodity trading, alongside Tether's investments in **[real-world asset tokenization](/posts/real-world-asset-tokenization)**. The company holds a substantial Bitcoin position of its own.

## How to Read Ardoino

Ardoino is the most consequential executive in crypto that most retail users have never heard of. He does not run an exchange like **[Coinbase](/exchanges/coinbase)** or **[Binance](/exchanges/binance)**, and he did not invent a protocol. He runs the dollar substitute that the rest of the market settles in, and he has been more forthcoming about it than anyone who held the job before him.

The fair assessment has two halves that both need stating. Tether under Ardoino is materially more transparent than Tether before him, holds a reserve portfolio far more conservative than the one regulators objected to, and has built something genuinely useful for people whose alternative was not a dollar bank account but no dollar access at all. It also carries a 2021 regulatory finding that its reserve claims were misleading, still lacks a full independent audit, and is large enough that its failure would be a systemic event rather than a company event.

For anyone holding USDt, the practical implication is that a stablecoin is a credit exposure to its issuer, not cash. It behaves like a dollar until it does not, and **[crypto volatility](/posts/crypto-volatility)** in the underlying market is a separate risk from the solvency of the token you are measuring it in. That is worth understanding regardless of how much confidence Ardoino's disclosures inspire.
