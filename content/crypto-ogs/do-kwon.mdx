Do Kwon is the South Korean entrepreneur who co-founded Terraform Labs and designed TerraUSD and LUNA, the paired tokens whose collapse in May 2022 erased roughly USD 40 billion in value and triggered the contagion that took down Three Arrows Capital, Celsius, Voyager and ultimately contributed to the failure of FTX. He pleaded guilty to conspiracy to defraud and wire fraud in August 2025 and was sentenced to 15 years in federal prison on December 11, 2025.

## What TerraUSD Was Supposed to Do

TerraUSD, or UST, was an algorithmic stablecoin. Unlike a stablecoin backed by dollars in a bank account, UST held its peg through a mint-and-burn arbitrage with its sister token LUNA.

The mechanism worked like this. Anyone could always exchange one UST for one dollar's worth of LUNA, and vice versa. If UST traded below a dollar, arbitrageurs could buy it cheaply, redeem it for a full dollar of LUNA, and profit, which removed UST from supply and pushed the price back up. If UST traded above a dollar, the trade ran the other way. No reserves were required. The peg was supposed to be maintained by self-interest and code.

Demand for UST came largely from Anchor Protocol, which offered roughly 20 percent annual yield on UST deposits. That rate was not generated by lending activity. It was subsidised from a reserve, and the reserve was being depleted. The yield was, in effect, marketing spend for a stablecoin, and it attracted billions in deposits from people who believed they had found a safe dollar asset paying twenty percent.

Anyone examining the arithmetic could see the problem. The entire structure depended on LUNA holding enough market value to absorb a redemption wave, and LUNA's value depended on confidence in UST. The two assets backed each other, which means neither was backed by anything.

## The Death Spiral

In early May 2022 large UST withdrawals broke the peg. The mechanism then did exactly what it was designed to do, and that was the disaster.

Holders redeemed UST for LUNA, which minted enormous quantities of new LUNA, which collapsed LUNA's price, which reduced the value available to absorb the next redemption, which accelerated the next wave. LUNA's supply went from roughly 350 million tokens to more than six trillion within days. Both assets went to effectively zero inside a week.

Kwon attempted a defence using a Bitcoin reserve the Luna Foundation Guard had accumulated, selling **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** into a falling market. It did not work, and it contributed to broader **[crypto volatility](/posts/crypto-volatility)** as billions in BTC hit the order books during a panic.

## The Lie at the Centre of the Case

The fraud Kwon was convicted of is narrower and more specific than the design failure, and the distinction matters.

UST had briefly lost its peg once before, in May 2021. Kwon told the world that the algorithm had automatically restored it, presenting this as proof the mechanism worked under stress. It was the single most important claim in Terra's investor narrative, and it was false. Kwon had privately arranged for a high-frequency trading firm to buy millions of dollars of UST to prop the price back up.

That is the heart of the criminal case. Designing a fragile financial product is not a crime. Telling investors that your product survived a stress test autonomously, when you in fact paid a trading firm to rescue it, is fraud. Every dollar that flowed into UST and Anchor after May 2021 did so partly on the strength of a stress test that had been staged.

Sentencing Kwon, Judge Paul Engelmayer described it as a fraud on an epic, generational scale, said Kwon had held an almost mystical hold over investors, and referred to incalculable human wreckage. He imposed 15 years, above the minimum of 12 the prosecution had requested.

## Arrest, Extradition and Sentence

The legal path was unusually long. Kwon left South Korea before the collapse was complete and was the subject of an Interpol red notice. He was arrested in Montenegro in March 2023 attempting to travel on a forged Costa Rican passport, and spent about 17 months in custody there while South Korea and the United States competed for extradition. He was eventually extradited to the US.

He pleaded guilty in August 2025 to conspiracy to defraud and wire fraud, and was sentenced on December 11, 2025. He received credit for time served in the United States and for the 17 months of pre-extradition custody.

Terraform Labs itself entered bankruptcy, and Kwon separately faced civil action from the SEC. South Korean authorities have their own proceedings. The 15-year sentence is among the longest imposed on a crypto executive, exceeding the terms given in several comparable cases though not that of **[Sam Bankman-Fried](/crypto-ogs/sam-bankman)**.

## What Terra Broke

The second-order damage exceeded the direct losses.

Three Arrows Capital, heavily exposed to Terra, failed within weeks, and its collapse propagated through the lenders that had funded it: Celsius, Voyager, BlockFi and Genesis. That chain of failures drained liquidity across the industry through 2022 and set the conditions in which FTX's own shortfall became unsurvivable. Exchanges including **[Binance](/exchanges/binance)**, **[Upbit](/exchanges/upbit)** and **[Bithumb](/exchanges/bithumb)** delisted the tokens and faced questions about why they had listed and promoted them.

Retail losses were severe and concentrated in South Korea, where Terra had been marketed hardest and where UST's yield was treated by many holders as a savings product. Reports of suicides followed. This is the part of the story that risk-adjusted-return discussion tends to omit.

Regulators responded quickly. Terra is the most cited case in the drafting of stablecoin rules worldwide, including in the European Union's MiCA framework and the US GENIUS Act, both of which require reserve backing that algorithmic designs cannot satisfy.

## What the Case Established

Three things are worth carrying away, and none of them is that algorithmic stablecoins are impossible in principle.

The first is that yield has to come from somewhere. Anchor's 20 percent was paid from a depleting reserve, which is a fact that was publicly available the entire time. **[Doing your own research](/posts/do-your-own-research)** on any yield product means identifying who is paying it and why, and a subsidy is not a business model.

The second is that reflexive collateral is not collateral. UST was backed by LUNA and LUNA was valued on confidence in UST. Any structure where asset A backs asset B and asset B underwrites asset A has no floor, and this is checkable from a whitepaper without waiting for a collapse.

The third is the one the sentence actually punished. Kwon was not imprisoned for building something that failed. He was imprisoned for telling investors the mechanism had proven itself when he had quietly paid to rescue it. The lesson is about founder claims rather than about **[blockchain](/posts/what-is-blockchain)** design: when a project's core evidence of robustness is an anecdote supplied by the project, it is not evidence.

Kwon was, before the collapse, among the most confident and combative figures in the industry, publicly dismissive of critics who identified precisely the flaw that destroyed him. He mocked them by name and dared them to short his tokens. That is the most durable detail in the whole affair, and the reason his case is taught rather than merely remembered: the warnings were specific, public and correct well before May 2022, and the confidence of the person being warned was treated by the market as a better signal than the arithmetic.
