## **Tom Lee: The Wall Street Strategist Who Bet on Ethereum**

Tom Lee is an American market strategist, co-founder of the research firm Fundstrat Global Advisors, and chairman of BitMine Immersion Technologies, the company that holds more Ether than any other on earth. He spent two decades as one of Wall Street's best-known equity strategists before becoming, in 2017, one of the first of them to publish formal research on **[Bitcoin](/posts/what-is-bitcoin)** for institutional clients.

In June 2025 he moved from forecasting crypto to owning a great deal of it. Under his chairmanship BitMine turned itself into a public company built around one asset, **[Ethereum](/posts/what-is-ethereum)**, and by 4 October 2026 it held 6,016,414 ETH, close to 5 percent of all the Ether in existence. That makes Lee one of the most consequential individual voices in the Ethereum market, and one of its most debated.

## **Early Life and Wall Street Career**

Thomas Jong Lee was born in 1969 in Westland, Michigan, the third of four children of Korean immigrant parents. His father was a psychiatrist; his mother, a homemaker, later ran a Subway franchise. He studied economics at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, with concentrations in finance and accounting, and later earned the CFA charter.

He began his career in the early 1990s as an equity research analyst covering telecommunications and wireless companies, first at Kidder, Peabody & Company and then at Salomon Smith Barney. He joined J.P. Morgan in 1999 and served as its chief equity strategist from 2007 to 2014. Institutional Investor ranked him among the top analysts in its annual surveys every year from 1998 onward.

His research was not always welcome. In 2002 the wireless carrier Nextel publicly attacked his coverage of the company, a dispute that drew a story in the Wall Street Journal. It was an early sign of a trait that would define his later career: a willingness to take a strong, public position and hold it under pressure.

In 2014 he left J.P. Morgan and co-founded Fundstrat Global Advisors, an independent research firm, where he became head of research. He also became a regular presence on CNBC, Bloomberg and Fox Business, which is where most retail investors first encountered him.

## **Entry into Crypto**

Lee's turn to crypto came in 2017, when Fundstrat began publishing formal Bitcoin research for institutional clients. At the time almost no major strategist would touch the asset. In early 2018 CNBC described him as the only major US stock market strategist issuing Bitcoin price targets at all.

His first targets were specific and public. In August 2017 he set a mid-2018 target of 6,000 dollars, raised it to 11,500 dollars that November, and laid out a longer call for 25,000 dollars by 2022. He framed Bitcoin as a competitor to gold, arguing it could cannibalise part of gold's role as a store of value.

The 2018 crash tested that conviction hard. Bitcoin fell by more than half from its December 2017 peak, and Lee kept issuing bullish targets through the slide, including a 20,000 dollar mid-2018 call that missed by a wide margin. Fundstrat eventually stopped publishing short-term Bitcoin price targets. The long-range 25,000 dollar call, however, was reached in late 2020, more than a year ahead of schedule.

## **Calls, Misses and the Permabull Label**

Lee's public reputation rests on two kinds of call, and it is worth separating them.

His broad directional calls on markets have often been right. In March 2020, as Covid-19 sent stocks into free fall, he told clients to buy, and the rebound that followed earned him a Wall Street Journal profile in 2021. His long-run view that Bitcoin would become an institutional asset was vindicated by the arrival of spot **[crypto ETFs](/posts/crypto-etfs-explained)** in the United States in 2024.

His specific price targets have a far weaker record. In December 2021 he said Bitcoin could reach 200,000 dollars in 2022; it fell below 16,000 dollars that November instead, in one of the deepest **[bear markets](/posts/bear-market-in-crypto)** the asset has seen. He spent much of 2025 promoting a 250,000 dollar target for the year's end, then backed away from it in November 2025 after Bitcoin peaked near 126,000 dollars in October.

That gap is why critics call him a permabull, and why his calls attract as much mockery as attention. Lee's defenders argue that he is paid to identify direction rather than timing, and that being early on Bitcoin was worth more to his clients than any single missed target cost them. Both readings are fair. Anyone using his forecasts should weigh them as a statement of conviction, not a measurement.

He has also drawn criticism outside markets. In September 2020 CNN cited his retweets of posts by the commentator James Todaro as an example of alleged coronavirus misinformation.

## **Fundstrat and the Granny Shots ETF**

Fundstrat remained the base of his business through the 2020s. In November 2024 the firm's asset management arm, Fundstrat Capital, where Lee is chief investment officer, launched the Fundstrat Granny Shots US Large Cap ETF under the ticker GRNY. The actively managed fund holds 20 to 50 large US companies chosen across several themes Fundstrat expects to drive the economy, and the firm said it passed 4 billion dollars in assets in January 2026.

The fund matters to his crypto story for one reason. It made Lee a manager of outside money in his own name, not just a commentator, and the credibility that came with it is part of what made BitMine's fundraising possible.

## **BitMine and the Ethereum Treasury**

On 30 June 2025, BitMine Immersion Technologies, a small Bitcoin mining company, announced a roughly 250 million dollar private placement at 4.50 dollars per share to fund a new strategy: holding Ether as its primary treasury asset. Lee became chairman of the board the same day. The round was led by MOZAYYX, with Founders Fund, Pantera, Galaxy Digital and Lee himself among the participants.

The template was explicit. Lee compared the plan to what **[Michael Saylor](/crypto-ogs/michael-saylor)** had done with Bitcoin at Strategy, arguing that Ethereum would capture the growth of **[stablecoins](/posts/what-are-stablecoins)** and **[real world asset tokenization](/posts/real-world-asset-tokenization)** the way Bitcoin had captured the store-of-value narrative. BitMine would raise money by selling shares and buy ETH with it, measuring success by ETH held per share.

He gave the goal a name: the "alchemy of 5%", owning 5 percent of the total Ether supply of about 120.7 million. BitMine bought at a pace few expected. It held about 3.4 million ETH by November 2025, 4.2 million in January 2026, and 5.74 million by July 2026. As of 4 October 2026 it reported 6,016,414 ETH, valued at about 16.4 billion dollars at 2,726 dollars per ETH, and described itself as about 1 percent short of the target.

Unlike Bitcoin, Ether can earn a yield. As of the same date BitMine had 5,067,309 ETH staked through its MAVAN platform, which reported a seven-day annualised yield of 2.63 percent. At the January 2026 shareholder meeting Lee said the company expected more than 400 million dollars a year in pre-tax income from its holdings, turning **[crypto staking](/posts/crypto-staking)** into the company's operating business. The yield exists because Ethereum secures itself through **[proof of stake](/posts/what-is-proof-of-stake)**, paying holders who lock up ETH to validate the network.

![A gold Ethereum coin standing upright amid falling gold coins](/images/posts/ethereum-network.jpg)

BitMine was not the only company to make this bet. **[Joseph Lubin](/crypto-ogs/joseph-lubin)**, an Ethereum co-founder, became chairman of SharpLink Gaming in 2025 with a similar ETH treasury plan, and for a while the two firms raced for the title of largest holder. BitMine pulled decisively ahead. Lee also spread the model beyond Ethereum: in September 2025 BitMine invested 20 million dollars in Eightco Holdings, which was adopting Worldcoin as its treasury asset, and Lee joined its board. In January 2026 BitMine committed 200 million dollars to Beast Industries, the company behind the YouTuber MrBeast.

## **Criticism of the Treasury Model**

BitMine's rise has been accompanied by sharp criticism, most of it aimed at the mechanics rather than at Ethereum.

The first is dilution. A company that buys ETH by selling stock grows its holdings partly by shrinking each existing shareholder's slice, and BitMine's share count rose steeply as it bought. That only benefits shareholders while the stock trades at a premium to the value of the ETH behind it. When the premium collapses, issuing shares to buy ETH stops creating value, and Lee has said BitMine will not issue shares below its own estimate of net asset value.

The second is timing. Ether reached a record near 4,950 dollars in August 2025, in the middle of BitMine's buying spree, and much of the treasury was bought well above the October 2026 price of 2,726 dollars. Reports from the January 2026 shareholder meeting put the company's unrealised losses at roughly 2.3 billion dollars at that point. Lee has described drawdowns as a feature rather than a bug of the strategy, an argument that did not stop the stock from falling with ETH through much of 2026.

The third is whether the model has a future at all. The short seller Kerrisdale Capital argued that BitMine was copying a Saylor playbook that was on its way to extinction. Lee's answer is that the company compounds ETH per share and earns staking income on top. In BitMine's 5 October 2026 update he noted that in the first nine months of 2026 ETH had fallen 10 percent while BMNR fell only 3 percent.

## **Philosophy and Public Persona**

Lee's case for crypto has always been institutional rather than ideological. He does not frame Bitcoin or Ethereum as an escape from the financial system, the way earlier crypto pioneers did. He frames them as assets the financial system will eventually need to own, and his career has been a series of attempts to be early to that adoption, first as an analyst, then as a fund manager, and now as the chairman of a company that is itself one of the largest holders.

That framing explains his particular enthusiasm for Ethereum. In his view the growth of stablecoins, tokenized stocks and on-chain settlement, accelerated in the United States by the **[GENIUS Act](/posts/what-is-the-genius-act)**, runs mostly on Ethereum, which makes Ether the asset most directly exposed to Wall Street moving on-chain. He has argued that **[Vitalik Buterin](/crypto-ogs/vitalik-buterin)**'s network is to tokenized finance what Bitcoin is to digital gold.

He remains highly visible. In September 2026 he told CoinDesk he expected the following 12 months to be "really bullish" for crypto, arguing that the four-year cycle tied to **[the Bitcoin halving](/posts/the-bitcoin-halving)** would bottom in October 2026. On 7 October 2026 he gave a keynote at Token2049 in Singapore.

## **Legacy and Influence**

Lee's lasting contribution may turn out to be less about any forecast than about permission. In 2017 he made it acceptable for a mainstream Wall Street strategist to take Bitcoin seriously in front of institutional clients, years before most of his peers did. That helped open the door for the ETFs and public-company treasuries that followed.

BitMine is the larger and less settled part of his record. In little more than a year he took a small mining company to roughly 5 percent of the Ether supply, a concentration of ownership with no real precedent among large crypto assets. Whether that becomes the Ethereum equivalent of Saylor's Bitcoin bet, or a cautionary example of buying at the top with shareholders' money, depends on where Ether goes from here. Few people in crypto have tied their reputation more tightly to a single price chart.
